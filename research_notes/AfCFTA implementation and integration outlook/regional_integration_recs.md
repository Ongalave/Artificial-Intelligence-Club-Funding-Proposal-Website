## Q1. What drove the push for integration at each historical stage, and what explains the gap between ambitious treaties and thin implementation?

### Takeaway
Continental integration was designed top-down: self-reliance in the Lagos Plan of Action (1980), then the Abuja Treaty's (1991) six-stage march to a common market and monetary union with the RECs as building blocks, then AU-era programmes (BIAT, PIDA, Agenda 2063, AfCFTA). Each target date has slipped. The sourced explanations are overlapping memberships, weak institutional capacity, disagreement over common external tariffs, non-tariff barriers (NTBs), narrow commodity-based production structures, political rivalry, unpaid dues and infrastructure or payment weaknesses.

### Cited Findings

**Continental initiatives (a)**
- The Lagos Plan of Action and Final Act of Lagos were adopted by Heads of State at the OAU's Second Extraordinary Session in Lagos in April 1980. The LPA described Africa as entering the 1980s as the "least developed region in the world", declared 1980-1990 the "Industrial Development Decade in Africa", aimed to reduce Africa's links with Western countries by maximising its own resources, and prioritised replacing imported food. — [Wikipedia: Lagos Plan of Action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_Plan_of_Action); [OAU LPA text (RESAKSS PDF)](https://www.resakss.org/sites/default/files/OAU%201980%20Lagos%20Plan%20of%20Action%20for%20the%20Economic%20Development%20of%20Africa.pdf)
- The Lagos Plan and Final Act reaffirmed a commitment to create an African Economic Community (AEC) by 2000. This was followed by the 1991 Treaty establishing the AEC, which proceeds in six stages with RECs as building blocks. — [SciELO: Towards the African Economic Community: Legal and Historical Perspectives](https://www.scielo.org.za/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S1727-37812023000100012); [Treaty text (McGill PTAs)](https://ptas.mcgill.ca/Agreements%20pdfs/Treaty%20establishing%20the%20African%20Economic%20Community.htm)
- The Abuja Treaty was signed on 3 June 1991 and entered into force on 12 May 1994. It sets a gradual process "in 6 stages over 34 years, by 2028" (search-summary paraphrase; the page itself was not opened). — [AU working doc "Fast-tracking the CFTA: RECs as building blocks"](https://www.au.int/web/sites/default/files/newsevents/workingdocuments/31409-wd-fast_tracking_the_cfta_-_recs_as_building_blocks_-_presentation_by_prudence.pdf); [Wikipedia: Regional economic communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_economic_communities); [UNECA: Key Pillars of Africa's Regional Integration](https://archive.uneca.org/node/2075)
- The Abuja Treaty's six stages, as relayed by the search summary:
  1. 1994-1999: establish and strengthen RECs.
  2. 2000-2007: eliminate tariff barriers and NTBs within RECs.
  3. 2008-2017: establish FTAs and customs unions in each REC.
  4. 2018-2019: continental customs union and common external tariff.
  5. 2020-2023: African common market, including free movement of factors of production and right of establishment.
  6. 2024-2028: pan-African economic and monetary union, including an African Central Bank and a single currency.
  — same sources as above.
- The Boosting Intra-African Trade (BIAT) Action Plan was adopted by the AU Assembly in January 2012. It has seven clusters: trade policy, trade facilitation, productive capacity, trade-related infrastructure, trade finance, trade information and factor market integration. It incorporates the Action Plan for Accelerated Industrial Development of Africa (AIDA) and the Programme for Infrastructure Development in Africa (PIDA, 2012). Its aim was to raise intra-African trade from about 10-13% to 25% or more within a decade. — [tralac: Agenda 2063 resources](https://www.tralac.org/resources/by-region/14352-african-union-agenda-2063.html); [Agenda 2063 First Ten-Year Plan (UNEP-hosted PDF)](https://wedocs.unep.org/bitstream/handle/20.500.11822/20823/Agenda%202063%20-%20FIRST%20TEN%20YEAR%20PLAN%20%20%20September%20%202015.pdf?sequence=1&isAllowed=y)
- Agenda 2063's First Ten-Year Implementation Plan captured BIAT priorities and continental flagship projects aimed at adding value to commodities and removing barriers to intra-African trade. — same tralac and wedocs pages.

**Drivers and obstacles**
- ARIA literature (UNECA, ARIA II and IV): more than 80% of African countries' exports go to markets outside the continent. Production and export structures are geared to primary commodities (minerals, timber, coffee, cocoa), with little industrial capacity to supply diversified manufactures to regional markets. Decades of REC experimentation have not delivered significant intra-REC or intra-African trade. Macro-policy non-convergence and insufficient infrastructure hamper integration. — [UNECA ARIA IV](https://archive.uneca.org/node/488); [UNECA ARIA II](https://archive.uneca.org/node/493); [Africa Portal: Regional Integration and Trade in Africa (2023)](https://africaportal.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Regional-Integration-and-Trade-in-Africa_FINAL-FOR-PUBLISING.pdf)
- ARIA XI (launched 12 July 2025 by AUC/UNECA/AfDB) names these gaps: imperfect implementation of FTAs and customs unions, prolonged transition periods for harmonising national customs laws, concerns about a common external tariff's effect on national development, overlapping REC memberships, and weak institutional frameworks that slow policy harmonisation. It says NTBs and policy and standards coordination matter beyond tariffs. (Search-summary paraphrase of the report.) — [ARIA XI PDF (UNECA)](https://www.uneca.org/sites/default/files/fullpublicationfiles/ARIA%20XI_Book_EN_12June_rev1.pdf); [DD Customs Law summary of ARIA XI](https://ddcustomslaw.com/index.php?catid=29%3Ail-cms&id=1003%3Auneca-publishes-11th-edition-of-the-assessing-regional-integration-in-africa-aria-xi-report&lang=en&option=com_content&view=article)
- SADC: the RISDP planned an FTA by 2008 and a customs union by 2010. The customs union was postponed; a later commitment to 2013 was also missed, and there was no progress by 2022. The delay is attributed to SADC Secretariat capacity constraints. — [Globalex: Law of SADC](https://www.nyulawglobal.org/globalex/southern_african_development_community1.html); [Wikipedia: African Free Trade Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Free_Trade_Zone); [SADC customs union page](https://www.sadc.int/integration-milestones/customs-union)
- COMESA: a customs union was initially planned for December 2004 and delayed for lack of agreement on the level of common external protection. Members later agreed to launch by December 2008. — [COMESA in Brief (PDF)](https://www.comesa.int/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/COMESA-in-brief-FINAL-_web.pdf); [IMF: The COMESA experience](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/display/book/9781557757692/ch017.xml)
- AMU: deep Morocco-Algeria political disagreement, notably over Western Sahara, has blocked progress. The Algeria-Morocco land border has been closed since 1994. — [Fanack: AMU](https://fanack.com/morocco/politics-of-morocco/the-arab-maghreb-union/); [WEF: Maghreb Union](https://www.weforum.org/stories/2017/06/five-ways-to-make-maghreb-work/); [Conciliation Resources](https://www.c-r.org/accord/cross-border-peacebuilding/why-maghreb-matters-threats-opportunities-and-options-cross)
- EAC: a funding crisis. See Q3.
- WAPP: incomplete grid links, regulatory fragmentation and payment issues constrain it. See Q2.

### Inferences
- The pattern of slipped deadlines is consistent across the Abuja stages. Stage 3 (FTAs and customs unions in all RECs by 2017) was missed in SADC, COMESA, ECCAS and others. Stage 4 (a continental customs union and CET, 2018-19) has not started, and the AU instead launched the AfCFTA (a free trade area plus rules) in 2018-21. So the AfCFTA is effectively a re-sequencing of Abuja, not its fulfilment on schedule. This is my synthesis of the sourced stage list and the sourced REC status, not a quoted claim.
- The ACB/AMF/AIB targets (2028-2034) look unlikely to be met on the stated schedule. The only dated figures I found are from 2017 (zero AMF ratifications), so the current state needs checking.
- Treaty ambition has run ahead of enforcement capacity. Missed dates appear in four distinct places: SADC's capacity-constrained secretariat, COMESA's CET disagreements, the AMU's political rivalry, and the EAC's arrears.

## Q2. How do the major RECs compare in actual achievements (tariffs, customs unions, intra-regional trade shares, free movement, infrastructure and energy pools, one-stop border posts)?

### Takeaway
By intra-regional export share, SADC and EAC lead (about 15-21%), ECOWAS and COMESA are about 8-11%, and ECCAS is about 2% and the AMU under 5%. REC institutions differ more in design than in outcomes. The EAC and ECOWAS lead on free movement and integration scores, SADC on FTA tariff elimination and infrastructure/energy, ECOWAS and CEMAC/WAEMU on common currencies and common external tariffs, and the AMU is effectively dormant.

**ECOWAS (15 members until Jan 2025, 12 after the AES exit; see Q3)**

Five bands: 0% (essential social goods), 5% (primary necessity, raw and capital goods), 10% (intermediate goods), 20% (final consumption goods), 35% (specific goods for economic development, such as certain cereals including rice, tobacco, textiles, alcoholic beverages). The implementation strategy included dismantling highway checkpoints (the intent; harassment at checkpoints persists as a documented problem in the migration literature). — [Adepoju (GFMD) PDF: Operationalising the ECOWAS Free Movement Protocol](https://www.gfmd.org/sites/g/files/tmzbdl1801/files/documents/AdepojuS8.pdf); [Migration Institute: Clottey](https://www.migrationinstitute.org/files/events/clottey.pdf)

- ECOWAS reaffirmed in July 2026 that it will launch a single currency (the "eco") in 2027. Reports say it will proceed with "those who are ready" without waiting for all states to meet convergence criteria, and may launch without WAEMU. — [Citi Newsroom (Jul 2026)](https://www.citinewsroom.com/2026/07/ecowas-reaffirms-plans-to-launch-single-currency-in-2027/); [Financial Afrik (Aug 2025)](https://www.financialafrik.com/en/2025/08/06/the-ecowas-eco-to-be-launched-as-early-as-2027-the-ouattara-option-chosen/); [Ecofin Agency](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-finances/1702-52963-ecowas-eco-currency-may-launch-without-waemu-in-2027-push)
- West African Power Pool: the CLSG interconnector (Côte d'Ivoire-Liberia-Sierra Leone-Guinea) is 1,303 km of 225 kV line with 11 substations and 290 MW transfer capacity across two circuits. **SADC (FTA 2008)**
- The FTA was officially launched in August 2008 by 12 of the 15 SADC Member States at that time. **EAC**
- Monetary Union Protocol signed 30 November 2013 in Kampala, aiming at a single currency by 2024. The target was missed and reaffirmed as 2031. No partner state has met all four. The EAMI Act came into force on 1 July 2021, but the host state was still unselected. Intra-AMU trade is 5% or less of members' total trade. — [Wikipedia: Arab Maghreb Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Maghreb_Union); [Fanack](https://fanack.com/morocco/politics-of-morocco/the-arab-maghreb-union/); [Executive Magazine](https://www.executive-magazine.com/north-africa/trade-across-the-maghreb); [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/uae/a-maghreb-union-hasn-t-brought-its-members-closer-1.556580)

### Inferences
- The two measures of intra-REC trade give different rankings. On Measure A (intra-REC exports over the REC's total exports), SADC ≈ EAC > ECOWAS ≈ COMESA > ECCAS (about 2%). On Measure B (REC share of continental intra-African trade), SADC far exceeds the others and the EAC is small. Report writers should pick one measure and name it. The EAC's high Measure A but low Measure B reflects small economies trading heavily with each other, whereas SADC's Measure B reflects South Africa's weight.
- The ranking pattern (EAC and SADC highest, ECCAS and AMU lowest) matches a political-economy story: the REC with the thinnest trade has the most political rivalry (AMU), the most security fragility (ECCAS), or the narrowest export base (ECCAS and CEMAC are oil- and mineral-dependent). This is inference, not sourced.
- Blocs containing common-currency zones do not clearly show higher intra-regional trade than blocs without them: ECOWAS (which includes WAEMU) is at 10.6% and ECCAS (which includes CEMAC) at 1.8% in the UNCTAD 2016 figures, versus COMESA at 10.2% with no currency union. This weighs against a simple "currency union raises trade" story, but the data are a coarse comparison across whole RECs (the currency zones are only part of each), not a causal study.

### Gaps
- SACU and the Common Monetary Area (CMA: South Africa, Lesotho, Eswatini, Namibia): not researched, because the shared search budget ran out. Needs the SACU revenue-sharing formula, the rand peg, and SACU's role in AfCFTA (which submitted a bloc tariff offer; see Q5).
- EAC customs union (2005) and common market (2010) details, intra-EAC NTB data, South Sudan (2016) and Somalia (2024) accession dates, and the EAC's CET structure: not retrieved. The only EAC enlargement facts retrieved are the DRC's 2022 accession and the arrears data in Q3.
- IGAD and CEN-SAD: nothing retrieved beyond the ARII claim that IGAD is highest on regional infrastructure. No sourced data on CEN-SAD's membership, activity or status. IGAD's mandate (drought/security, free movement protocol, Kenya-Ethiopia corridors) is not sourced.
- COMESA customs union: launch date, current CET status and updated FTA participation figures were not retrieved (data found are from 2017).
- SAPP performance (competitive market share, trading volumes), EAPP and one-stop border posts (OSBPs): not retrieved. Only WAPP/CLSG was sourced.
- ECOWAS intra-REC trade for 2023-2025, ETLS compliance and CET implementation scorecards: not retrieved.
- Primary-source verification of ARII scores by REC (values for each dimension).
- **Unverified background:** SADC's current membership is 16 (Comoros joined 2017). COMESA now has about 21 members. CMA covers South Africa, Lesotho, Eswatini and Namibia (Namibia and Botswana have different arrangements). CEMAC members are Cameroon, Central African Republic, Chad, Republic of Congo, Equatorial Guinea and Gabon. IGAD was founded in 1996 (IGADD in 1986). CEN-SAD was founded 1998 in Tripoli. ## Q3. What has been the effect of the Sahel states' exit from ECOWAS and other recent REC tensions (EAC arrears, DRC-Rwanda conflict, SADC/EAC overlaps)?

### Takeaway
Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger left ECOWAS on 29 January 2025 and formed the AES confederation. In practice the rupture has been softened by transitional ECOWAS arrangements and talks on a "new framework" (mandate extended to December 2026), but the AES has levied a 0.5% import charge on ECOWAS goods, has no common currency, and remains in the WAEMU/CFA zone. The EAC is in a financial crisis (arrears above US$89m), and the DRC-Rwanda conflict, with both countries in the EAC (and the DRC also in SADC), continues despite the 2025 Washington and Doha agreements.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- ECOWAS has chosen managed divorce over sanctions. The 2024 exit and the January 2025 transitional measures kept ETLS access and free movement as a bridge, and talks continue to at least end-2026. Yet the AES's 0.5% levy on ECOWAS goods means that trade between the three landlocked states and the rest of ECOWAS is now taxed. How far the customs picture changed beyond that is unclear: the AES states remain in WAEMU, whose four tariff bands the ECOWAS CET was built on, so whether WAEMU's CET continues to apply to them is something I did not verify.
- The Sahel exit shrinks ECOWAS to 12 members (arithmetic from 15 less 3) and weakens the claim that ECOWAS is a coherent customs union plus movement regime. Because the AES states remain in WAEMU, the CFA zone, and likely the AfCFTA, an ECOWAS single currency in 2027 would not include them, and WAEMU's monetary link to Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger continues. Effects on trade volumes have not been quantified in my sources.
- EAC arrears show the same revenue and political-commitment gap as the treaty-implementation gap in Q1. States value membership but do not fund the Secretariat, and those with active conflicts (DRC, South Sudan, Somalia, Burundi) are the biggest defaulters.
- The DRC-Rwanda war puts two EAC members in direct conflict, and the DRC (also in SADC) is a link between the EAC-SADC overlap and the security question. The regional mediation role has shifted to external and AU actors.

- Exact ECOWAS transitional arrangement terms (deadline, conditions) and any ECOWAS-AES agreement reached after July 2026: not retrieved.
- AES-WAEMU relationship, the AES bank (Confederal Investment and Development Bank) and common currency plans: only a general statement of "no currency yet".
- SAMIDRC (SADC mission in DRC), its withdrawal, and SADC-EAC joint-summit mechanisms (the 2025 EAC-SADC joint process): not found in the results. This leaves the SADC/EAC overlap angle weakly covered.
- Other REC tensions (SADC internal, ECCAS/CEMAC governance, IGAD-Sudan war and Ethiopia-Eritrea tensions, ECOWAS-Guinea-Bissau and Benin coup events): not covered.
- **Unverified background:** my recollection is that ECOWAS set a transitional period running to mid-2025 for the AES states' trade and movement arrangements; whether it expired or was replaced by the current negotiations is unverified. SAMIDRC (SADC Mission in DRC) was, as I recall, deployed in December 2023 and withdrawn in 2025.

## Q4. What do academic and policy evaluations (UNECA ARIA, Afreximbank, World Bank, Brookings, ODI, ECDPM) conclude about the success of African regional integration?

### Takeaway
The consensus in the material I could retrieve is "partial and uneven success": regional frameworks exist and are improving on paper (ARIA XI, ARII, IIAG), but intra-African trade remains about 15% of total trade, product structures are narrow, NTBs and overlaps persist, and the greatest gains are expected from trade facilitation rather than tariff cuts (World Bank).

### Cited Findings
- UNECA ARIA series: assesses where integration succeeded or failed, including why intra-African trade stays low and how macro-policy divergence and weak infrastructure hamper integration. Africa's internal trade has been consistently small relative to extra-African trade (80%+ of exports leave the continent). Decades of integration experimentation have not produced significant intra-REC or intra-African trade. — [UNECA ARIA IV](https://archive.uneca.org/node/488); [UNECA ARIA II](https://archive.uneca.org/node/493); [AllAfrica (2004): ECA launches ARIA I](https://allafrica.com/stories/200407140745.html)
- ARIA XI (July 2025): "moderate but still incomplete" progress, with gains in institutional, infrastructure and trade areas driven by AfCFTA, PIDA and relatively solid REC legal frameworks. EAC, ECOWAS and SADC are top performers; implementation of FTAs and customs unions remains imperfect; overlaps and weak institutions slow harmonisation. — [ARIA XI PDF](https://www.uneca.org/sites/default/files/fullpublicationfiles/ARIA%20XI_Book_EN_12June_rev1.pdf); [UNECA story: ARIA XI progress](https://www.uneca.org/stories/experts-make-progress-on-forthcoming-11th-edition-on-assessing-regional-integration-in); [DD Customs Law summary](https://ddcustomslaw.com/index.php?catid=29%3Ail-cms&id=1003%3Auneca-publishes-11th-edition-of-the-assessing-regional-integration-in-africa-aria-xi-report&lang=en&option=com_content&view=article); [UNECA: AU/ECA/AfDB launch report](https://www.uneca.org/stories/african-union,-eca,-and-afdb-launch-landmark-report-on-advancing-africa%E2%80%99s-economic)
- ARII (older edition): trade integration was the highest-scoring dimension, with an average of 0.546 across RECs (search-summary paraphrase of the 2016 ARII PDF; edition uncertain). — [ARII 2016, section 1 (UNECA PDF)](https://archive.uneca.org/sites/default/files/PublicationFiles/arii-report2016-section1_en.pdf); [ARII 2016, section 2](https://archive.uneca.org/sites/default/files/PublicationFiles/arii-report2016-section2_en.pdf); [ARII 2019 report](https://indiaenvironmentportal.org.in/reports-and-documents/africa-regional-integration-index-report-2019-2)
- Afreximbank African Trade Report 2025: intra-African trade share at 15.3% in 2024 (14.7% in 2023); trade finance challenges highlighted. — [Afreximbank report PDF](https://media.afreximbank.com/afrexim/African-Trade-Report_2025.pdf); [Habari Network summary](https://www.thehabarinetwork.com/afreximbank-2025-trade-report-reveals-surge-in-intra-african-commerce-amid-global-shifts); [Further Africa summary](https://furtherafrica.com/2025/08/04/african-trade-report-2025/)
- World Bank (2020), *The African Continental Free Trade Area: Economic and Distributional Effects*: full AfCFTA implementation could raise real incomes by 7% by 2035 (nearly US$450bn, 2014 prices). Tariff liberalisation alone adds about 0.2% at continental level; tariffs plus NTB reduction about 2.4%; adding the Trade Facilitation Agreement lifts the gain to 7%. About 30 million people could leave extreme poverty. Country gains range from about 2% (Madagascar, Malawi, Mozambique) to 14% (Côte d'Ivoire, Zimbabwe). — [World Bank report text](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/216831595998182418/txt/The-African-Continental-Free-Trade-Area-Economic-and-Distributional-Effects.txt); [World Bank OKR](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/handle/10986/37623); [tralac summary](https://www.tralac.org/news/article/14808-afcfta-trade-pact-could-boost-africa-s-income-by-450-billion-study-finds.html)
- Brookings (regional value chains and AfCFTA) and Chatham House (Tanzania between EAC and SADC) were found but their conclusions were not extractable. — [Brookings: Strengthening regional value chains](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/strengthening-regional-value-chains-whats-the-role-of-the-african-continental-free-trade-area/); [Chatham House](https://www.chathamhouse.org/2024/04/reviving-tanzanias-regional-leadership-and-global-engagement/03-between-eac-and-sadc)

## Q5. How do RECs relate to the AfCFTA (building blocks, acquis, differing tariff schedules, variable geometry)?

### Takeaway
The AfCFTA Agreement builds on the RECs: REC FTAs are "building blocks", the acquis is preserved (Article 19(2) lets deeper REC integration continue among REC members), and variable geometry (Article 5) allows differentiated pace. In practice customs unions (CEMAC, EAC, ECOWAS, SACU) submitted tariff offers as blocs, rules of origin are now fully agreed (by Feb 2026), but only about 25 states had legally gazetted readiness to trade by March 2026.

### Inferences
- The AfCFTA is the continental free trade area that the Abuja Treaty placed only after REC-level FTAs and customs unions (stages 3-4). It was launched before those REC layers were complete (see Q1 inference). A tralac discussion piece on the AfCFTA and other AU initiatives covers this relationship: [tralac](https://www.tralac.org/discussions/article/12790-the-african-continental-free-trade-area-afcfta-and-other-african-union-au-initiatives-for-economic-integration.html) (content not extracted).
- The AES states left ECOWAS but are presumably still AfCFTA parties; I did not check each state's AfCFTA ratification or tariff offer.
- Customs unions submitted joint schedules, which means REC CETs act as the floor for the AfCFTA schedule of those blocs, while FTAs (SADC non-SACU, COMESA) submitted country-by-country. The AfCFTA therefore has two tracks, with ECOWAS, EAC, CEMAC and SACU as quasi-single offers. This is why the loss of ECOWAS cohesion in the Sahel complicates ECOWAS's own offer. The AES states are not part of the ECOWAS bloc offer after January 2025 (inference, unverified).
- Article 19(2) and the acquis mean the AfCFTA does not harmonise away the REC regimes: states with deeper REC integration keep it. But with only four customs unions and a Tripartite FTA that entered into force only in July 2024, the REC layer below the AfCFTA is itself incomplete (inference from the Q2 findings).
- Variable geometry has real use in the AfCFTA's phased tariff schedules (Categories A, B, C), but since no source I retrieved gives the category definitions or the exact liberalisation percentages, the report writer should source those separately.
