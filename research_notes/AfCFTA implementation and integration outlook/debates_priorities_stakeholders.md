## 1. What are governments, AU institutions, the AfCFTA Secretariat, development banks and business groups asking to be prioritized in 2026-2030?

### Takeaway
The dominant ask across governments, the AfCFTA Secretariat, Afreximbank, the AfDB and business is "implementation over negotiation". They want tariff preferences, customs, payments, logistics and finance made to work in practice. Named sector priorities are agriculture and agro-processing, pharmaceuticals, automotive, and transport/logistics. Digital trade and women/youth/MSME finance are also named, and the AU Assembly has added a new Protocol on Industrial Policy and Development to the negotiating agenda.

### Inferences
- The language of "implementation", "delivery" and "trucks, not treaties" is shared by the Secretariat, the Council chair (Nigeria), the AU Heads of State committee, Afreximbank and the AfDB. The real contest in 2026-2030 is therefore more likely over which implementation instruments (finance, customs, payments, industrial policy) and who pays for them, than over whether to implement.
- Leadership of the AfCFTA agenda is visibly concentrated in a few states in 2026: Kenya (Ruto chairs both the Heads of State implementation committee and the AU institutional-reform process), Nigeria (Council chair, IATF 2027 host), Egypt (outgoing Council chair, host of both the Afreximbank annual meetings and the MYCM) and Togo (Biashara Afrika host). This is an observation from the event record, not a documented claim by the sources.
- Ghana's President Mahama was elected First Vice-Chair of the AU in Feb 2026 ([AU](https://au.int/en/pressreleases/20260215/39th-ordinary-session-assembly-african-union-concludes)), which on the AU's rotation convention would put Ghana in line for the 2027 chair (inference; not stated in the sources).

## 2. Where is there broad consensus, and where do significant disagreements or competing interests remain?

### Takeaway
Consensus is strongest on the legal and enabling agenda: finalised rules of origin, implementation focus, trade facilitation/NTBs/logistics, payments (PAPSS), digital trade, finance and women/youth inclusion. The conflicts are in distribution and politics. They concern who liberalises how fast (tariff schedules and sensitive/exclusion lists), industrial-policy space versus harmonisation, compensation and LDC concerns, free movement, REC/AES fragmentation, the pull of external deals, and financing credibility. Documented evidence of specific national positions is thin in this session.

### Cited Findings

**A. Points of documented consensus / convergence**
1. **Rules of origin are now fully agreed.** In Feb 2026 the AU Assembly adopted the outstanding RoO for automotive and for clothing & textiles, so "100% of Rules of Origin are now agreed". — [UCT Trade Talks Africa](https://commerce.uct.ac.za/school-public-governance/articles/2026-03-12-clone-trade-talks-africa-policy-progress-and-people-behind-afcfta-issue-8). One search summary adds that the automotive RoO set a 40% local-content requirement (underlying source not isolated; verify) — [African Newspage](https://www.africannewspage.net/2026/02/39th-au-summit-afcfta-heads-of-state-implementation-committee-launched/).
2. **Shift from negotiation to implementation** is stated by the Secretariat, Nigeria as Council chair, the AU Assembly and Afreximbank (see section 1).
3. **Trade facilitation, NTBs, customs, logistics.** ECDPM's five priorities for AfCFTA-REC relations are: integrated customs processes, mutual recognition of standards, clearer treatment of domestic taxes and fees, stronger NTB reporting and resolution, and attention to geography, infrastructure and security. — [ECDPM](https://ecdpm.org/work/how-afcfta-regional-relations-can-support-continental-trade). The online NTB reporting/monitoring mechanism (Annex 5 of the Protocol on Trade in Goods) is the existing tool, with 2026 UNCTAD/Secretariat capacity-building (for example an Ethiopia workshop, 30-31 Mar 2026) — [AfCFTA NTB page](https://au-afcfta.org/operational-instruments/ntbs/). Mene's barrier list (section 1) overlaps with this.
4. **Payments and digital trade.** PAPSS expansion (section 1) and Mene's and Oduwole's digital-trade priorities. The Digital Trade Protocol is referenced as an enabler of data-centre and technology investment — [MyJoyOnline](https://www.myjoyonline.com/afcfta-needs-efficient-payment-systems-borders-and-infrastructure-to-boost-intra-african-trade-wamkele-mene/).
5. **Value chains** (agro-processing, pharmaceuticals, autos): Mene's sector list; the Council's "deepening regional value chains"; the AfDB's "Made in Africa, Marketed in Africa"; the WEF 2026 publication "AfCFTA Implementation: Learnings from Value Chains and Regional Blocs" (headline only) — [WEF](https://www.weforum.org/publications/afcfta-implementation-learnings-from-value-chains-and-regional-blocs/).
6. **Financing and adjustment**: Afreximbank's $1bn adjustment facility (older), Nigeria's $1bn window, the Ecobank-Secretariat partnership, the EU's €1.2bn Team Europe Initiative in support of African economic integration (80+ actions) — [EEAS](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/delegations/african-union-au/european-union-and-afcfta-secretariat-strengthen-their-partnership-promote-intra-african-trade-and_en); [EU trade policy page](https://policy.trade.ec.europa.eu/development-and-sustainability/economic-partnerships_en).
7. **Inclusion of women, youth and MSMEs** appears in Nigeria's four priorities, the AfDB's stated priorities, the union demands and the ECA project (section 1).
8. **Visa openness as a stated goal** is voiced by Mene and Dangote, but state practice is going the other way (see D5 below).

**B. Tariff liberalisation: depth and speed, sensitive/exclusion lists**
- Phase 1 design (tralac summary): 90% of tariff lines liberalised in 5 years (10 for LDCs, with a "G6" group — Ethiopia, Madagascar, Malawi, Sudan, Zambia, Zimbabwe — getting 15 years, an older modality); up to 7% of lines "sensitive" (phased over 10 years for developing and 13 for LDCs) and up to 3% excluded for food security, national security, fiscal revenue, livelihood and industrialisation. Category B phase-down began 1 Jan 2026. — [tralac AfCFTA resources](https://www.tralac.org/resources/by-region/cfta.html); [The Africa Report (older)](https://www.theafricareport.com/364284/afcfta-symbolic-advances-but-rough-road-slows-progress/)
- Implementation status: as of March 2026 only 25 countries had submitted gazette notices confirming domestic readiness to trade under AfCFTA preferential tariffs, and more than 40 countries were issuing AfCFTA certificates of origin. The Guided Trade Initiative grew from 8 founding participants to about 39 countries by mid-2025. — [Rio Times](https://www.riotimesonline.com/afcfta-status-2026/); [trade.gov GTI](https://www.trade.gov/market-intelligence/african-continental-free-trade-area-afcfta-guided-trade-initiative)
- A tralac-attributed summary says 50 tariff offers on goods had been submitted (including collective offers from the customs unions CEMAC, EAC, ECOWAS and SACU) and 48 verified. The summary dated this "as at July 2026", but the underlying document may be the July 2025 progress report, so the date is unclear (verify). Negotiations on sensitive/excluded lists were described as continuing. — [tralac progress report (July 2025)](https://www.tralac.org/documents/resources/cfta/7851-progress-report-of-the-african-continental-free-trade-area-july-2025/file.html)
- Industry/analyst commentary describes implementation as lagging politics: tariff schedules, domestication into national law, customs systems and certificates of origin lag the agreements, with NTBs, logistics and customs procedures the main constraints. — [Rio Times](https://www.riotimesonline.com/afcfta-status-2026/); [Financial Afrik on FIPE-UEMOA, 26 Jun 2026](https://www.financialafrik.com/en/2026/06/26/at-the-fipe-uemoa-experts-raise-concerns-about-obstacles-to-the-implementation-of-the-afcfta/) (headline only)

**C. Rules of origin stringency (autos, textiles)**
- Automotive and clothing/textile rules were the last two sectors resolved, only in Feb 2026. In Sept 2025 Financial Afrik was still reporting "obstacles" in those sectors. — [UCT Trade Talks Africa](https://commerce.uct.ac.za/school-public-governance/articles/2026-03-12-clone-trade-talks-africa-policy-progress-and-people-behind-afcfta-issue-8); [Financial Afrik, Sept 2025](https://www.financialafrik.com/en/2025/09/06/afcfta-rules-of-origin-facing-obstacles-in-the-automotive-and-textile-sectors/) (headline only; older). Which countries pushed which positions was not retrieved.

**D. Developmental regionalism vs tariff liberalisation; industrial policy**
- The Feb 2026 mandate for an industrial-policy protocol is the formal opening of this debate. A Policy Center for the New South paper by Luladay Berhanu Mengistie and Guillaume Gérout Suominen (21 Sept 2026) is described as a conceptual mapping exercise. It seeks a definition of industrial policy narrow enough for treaty language but broad enough to capture the instruments that drive industrialisation. It sets out what is realistically negotiable: shared principles, a safe-harbour catalogue of permitted instruments, positive harmonisation in targeted areas, and governance/coordination mechanisms, and it discusses the negotiating dynamics that will decide which gets binding treatment. — [Policy Center for the New South](https://www.policycenter.ma/publications/how-negotiate-industrial-policy-under-afcfta-architecture-coherence-and-governance)
- Dangote and the Afreximbank/AfDB framings (industrialisation, "economic sovereignty", "keep capital at home") lean developmental. Brookings' Foresight Africa 2026 promotes corridor-based "good jobs zones" and "enforceable labor standards" (search summary). — [Brookings Foresight Africa 2026](https://www.brookings.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Foresight-Africa-2026-FULL-REPORT.pdf)

**E. Revenue loss, LDC and "dumping ground" concerns**
- Concern that the least industrialised markets will be "flooded", and that intra-African "dumping" could replace imbalances with developed economies, is documented in secondary commentary (older and undated results). — [ISS African Futures AfCFTA page](https://futures.issafrica.org/thematic/08-afcfta/)
- Nigerian manufacturers' fear that Nigeria could become a dumping ground absent strict rules-of-origin enforcement (2021, older) — [Vanguard, 2021](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2021/04/afcfta-concern-over-dumping-mounts-as-trade-deficit-widens/). The AfCFTA has among the greatest income disparities of any continental FTA, more than double ASEAN's or CARICOM's (older commentary) — [ISS African Futures](https://futures.issafrica.org/thematic/08-afcfta/)
- Compensation: the Adjustment Fund's estimated $10bn need versus the $1bn Afreximbank facility (see section 1) is the documented gap; whether any customs-dependent LDC has drawn on it was not found. **F. Free movement of persons and visas**
- The Protocol on Free Movement of Persons, Right of Residence and Right of Establishment has been signed by 32 countries but ratified by only four (Mali, Niger, Rwanda, São Tomé and Príncipe) against 15 needed to enter into force. — [Africa Visa Openness](https://www.visaopenness.org/pages-not-in-use/latest-developments-on-the-afcfta/). **G. RECs, overlapping membership, AES**
- Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger (AES) left ECOWAS effective 29 Jan 2025 but remain AfCFTA members. They introduced a 0.5% levy on imports from several West African countries including Nigeria. In Oct 2025 they met in Bamako on a common external tariff and an AES customs code. A planned new AES currency lagged as of 2026, with the bloc still using the CFA franc inside WAEMU. — [BusinessDay NG](https://businessday.ng/maritime/article/sahel-blocs-import-tariff-on-ecowas-threatens-west-africa-trade-afcfta/); [Rio Times](https://www.riotimesonline.com/sahel-aes-confederation-economy-2026/); [Habari Network](https://www.thehabarinetwork.com/alliance-of-sahel-states-moves-toward-a-customs-union-a-new-model-for-african-economic-integration); [Africa Index (June 2026)](https://africaindex.com/blog/aes-confederation-two-year-scorecard-june-2026)
- The CRS noted that, of the eight AU-recognised RECs, only EAC and ECOWAS were operational customs unions with a common external tariff (older; ECOWAS's position is now complicated by the AES exit). — [CRS IF11423](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF11423)
- ECOWAS Parliament wants ECOWAS to "lead, coordinate and harmonize" AfCFTA implementation (section 1); ECDPM argues roles between AfCFTA and regional bodies still need to be shaped (headline-level). — [ECDPM](https://ecdpm.org/work/how-afcfta-regional-relations-can-support-continental-trade)

**H. Role of the AU Commission vs the AfCFTA Secretariat**
- Legally, the Secretariat is "functionally autonomous" within the AU system with independent legal personality, but its budget comes from the overall AU budget approved by the Assembly (tralac legal analysis, older). — [tralac](https://www.tralac.org/blog/article/15670-legal-aspects-of-the-relationship-between-the-afcfta-and-the-african-union.html). **I. Customs union / common external tariff / monetary union**
- The Abuja Treaty's original timeline (1991) envisaged a continental customs union by 2019 and a common market by 2023, so those milestones were missed. The AfCFTA is described as laying "the groundwork" for a customs and monetary union. — [CRS R47197](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R47197); [CRS IF11423](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF11423). The 2034 target named in the assignment was not confirmed in any retrieved source.

**J. Financing credibility (member-state/donor/Africa-owned banks)**
- Afreximbank has been in a dispute over "preferred creditor status" in the Ghana and Zambia debt restructurings; the Paris Club reportedly viewed those loans as commercial. Afreximbank said in Dec 2025 it had reached an agreement with Ghana on a $750m loan, while Zambia remained unresolved. It severed ties with Fitch after a downgrade to junk (Jan 2026). S&P restored its investment-grade rating in June 2026. — [BusinessDay (SA), 25 Jan 2026](https://www.businessday.co.za/world/2026-01-25-rating-cut-spurs-afreximbank-to-cut-ties-with-fitch/); [Polity, 12 Jun 2026](https://polity.org.za/article/sp-restores-afreximbanks-investment-grade-rating-2026-06-12) (headline only); [Afronomicslaw](https://www.afronomicslaw.org/category/analysis/preferred-principle-penalised-practice-afreximbank-and-politics-preferred) (headline only)

**K. Institutions and siting**
- The Council launched the process to choose a host country for the AfCFTA Competition Authority and Tribunal (section 1); no list of bidders was found.

### Inferences
- The G6 15-year phase-down, the 3% exclusion basket and the Adjustment Fund's size gap together suggest the "dumping-ground" argument is being handled through carve-outs and slow phase-downs rather than resolved. This is why only 25 states had gazetted preferential tariffs by March 2026, even with RoO complete. That causal link is an inference.
- The new industrial-policy protocol is likely to be the main arena where "developmental regionalism" (policy space, local content, safe-harbour instruments) is tested against liberalisers' concerns. The Policy Center paper's emphasis on a "safe-harbour catalogue" suggests the draft will probably avoid hard bans on subsidies and local-content rules. This reads the paper's framing and does not report negotiating positions.
- "Deepen the AfCFTA before a common currency" has no documented counter-camp in the sources retrieved. The evidence is that currency/customs-union integration inside the AES is stalling and that continental customs-union milestones have been missed. Treat "consensus on sequencing" as the researcher's inference, not documentation.
- Free movement is a classic case of rhetorical consensus with contrary behaviour: leaders and business endorse openness while ratifications are 4 of 15 and visa requirements are rising.

## 3. How have external shocks reshaped the debate (US tariffs, AGOA, debt/aid, China zero-tariff, EU EPAs/CBAM, Gulf/Turkey)?

### Takeaway
External shocks have pushed two narratives at once. One says the AfCFTA is the hedge ("keep capital and trade at home", "economic sovereignty"). The other says African states are still racing for bilateral access deals (AGOA, China, EU) that can cut across continental integration. AGOA was saved only in short-term form, passing through a one-year renewal in Feb 2026 and then a 2028 extension signed in Sept 2026. China's non-reciprocal zero-tariff offer took effect on 1 May 2026.

### Cited Findings

**US tariffs**
- On 20 Feb 2026 the US Supreme Court held that IEEPA does not authorise tariffs. A 10% global tariff under Section 122 took effect 24 Feb and was set to expire 24 July. A US Court of International Trade ruling in May 2026 found the Section 122 tariffs unlawful, but its practical impact was limited because the injunction covered only specific plaintiffs. — [CRS LSB11398](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/LSB11398); [CSIS "State of Play"](https://www.csis.org/analysis/state-play-global-impact-scotus-decision-ieepa-tariffs); [Skadden, May 2026](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/05/us-trade-court-strikes-down-section-122-tariffs)
- Country effects (secondary sources): South Africa's rate fell from 30% to 10% for goods not already under Section 232 or exempt, but its auto-parts sector still faced high effective rates. Lesotho had gone from 50% (April 2025) to a negotiated 15%, still worse than Kenya and Eswatini at 10%. AGOA exports fell 32% in the year to Nov 2025 versus 2024. — [allAfrica, 13 Mar 2026](https://allafrica.com/stories/202603130385.html); [Uganda Business News, Apr 2026](https://ugbusiness.com/2026/04/politics-policy/trump-tariffs-blunt-impact-of-agoa-renewal-for-african-exporters); [tariffstool (low-quality aggregator; verify)](https://www.tariffstool.com/tariffs-from-south-africa)
- tralac has an analysis "US Tariff Actions Against Africa 2025-26: Some Regional and Sectoral Insights" (headline only) — [tralac](https://www.tralac.org/blog/article/17084-us-tariff-actions-against-africa-2025-26-some-regional-and-sectoral-insights.html); CGD has "Tariff Whiplash in Africa: What Happened and What's to Come" (headline only) — [CGD](https://www.cgdev.org/publication/tariff-whiplash-africa-what-happened-and-whats-come).
- What happened to US tariff policy on Africa after the Section 122 expiry date (24 July 2026) was not found.

**AGOA**
- Sequence: House passed the AGOA Extension Act (H.R. 6500) on 12 Jan 2026 (340-54). A retroactive one-year renewal (through 31 Dec 2026, "the shortest authorization in the program's history") was signed 3 Feb 2026 inside a spending package. A further extension through 31 Dec 2028 then passed (Senate 90-6; House 370-48 on 1 Sept 2026) and was signed 2 Sept 2026 as Public Law 119-103 inside a funding bill. — [Congress.gov H.R. 6500](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/6500); [The Exchange Africa](https://theexchange.africa/agoa-renewal-2026-status/); [Semafor, 2 Sep 2026](https://www.semafor.com/article/09/02/2026/us-africa-trade-pact-extended); [AmCham SA](https://amcham.co.za/news/us-africa-trade-pact-extended); [USTR statement, Feb 2026](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/february/statement-ambassador-jamieson-greer-reauthorization-african-growth-and-opportunity-act). Several summaries conflict on "three years" versus "two years" and on the Senate vote date (summaries say 8 Aug); the one-year-then-2028 sequence best fits the evidence (verify exact dates).
- Coverage: about 1,800 tariff lines, 33 eligible countries, the third-country fabric provision retained; African exports under AGOA exceeded $30bn in 2024, with apparel above $1.4bn. A summary says Congress directed the executive to seek reciprocal trade and investment negotiations with AGOA countries, but the 2028 extension added no new reciprocity conditions (verify). — [Congress.gov/CRS](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF10149); [Fibre2Fashion](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/news/textile-news/trump-signs-into-law-h-r-6500-2-yr-extension-for-hope-help-acts-313211-newsdetails.htm); [FDD, May 2026 "Modernization of AGOA"](https://www.fdd.org/analysis/2026/05/12/modernization-of-the-african-growth-and-opportunity-act/) (headline only)
- Reaction: CSIS's Oge Onubogu says the extension "does not provide the certainty" African businesses and governments need and asks "what is the US' long-term economic vision for Africa?" South Africa had lobbied for a 15-year extension. — [Briefly SA](https://briefly.co.za/world/us/252089-us-extends-agoa-trade-deal-2028-securing-duty-free-access-sub-saharan-africa/); [EWN, 9 Sep 2026](https://www.ewn.co.za/2026/09/09/america-extends-agoa-but-sa-investment-uncertainty-remains)
- "AGOA-AfCFTA politics: Why Africa rallies for access, hesitates at integration" is a Zawya analysis (headline only; content not seen) — [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/economy/africa/agoa-afcfta-politics-why-africa-rallies-for-access-hesitates-at-integration-kez3jhtu).

- EU-ESA: in June 2026 negotiations on an enhanced EPA with Comoros, Madagascar, Mauritius and Seychelles were concluded, reported as the first fully fledged EU free trade agreement with Sub-Saharan partners, covering services, investment and digital trade. — [Rio Times](https://www.riotimesonline.com/eu-africa-trade-deal-indian-ocean-epa-2026/) (aggregator; verify against an EU source)

**Debt, aid, Gulf, Turkey**
- The AU Assembly (Feb 2026) cited debt restructuring and sustainable development financing among priorities for coordinated African positions (section 1). The Afreximbank preferred-creditor dispute (section 2J) shows debt politics reaching Africa's own trade-finance institutions.
- AU Commission-UAE joint statement (6 Jan 2026) exists (headline only) — [AU](https://au.int/en/pressreleases/20260106/joint-statement-between-african-union-commission-and-united-arab-emirates). No content on Gulf or Turkish investment/CEPAs was retrieved.

### Inferences
- The US shock appears to have changed the argument in two directions. It strengthened the rhetoric of continental self-reliance (Afreximbank's "economic sovereignty"; Dangote; ISS-type arguments that the AfCFTA is the long-run hedge) and also strengthened incentives for bilateral deal-making (Kenya's dual track; South Africa's AGOA lobbying). Neither is documented as a formal AU position.
- Because the 2028 AGOA deadline falls inside the 2026-2030 horizon and the 2026 renewal sequence was last-minute, AGOA-dependent apparel and auto-parts exporters (Lesotho, Madagascar, Kenya, South Africa) are likely to keep favouring bilateral US access over deeper AfCFTA concessions (inference from the sector exposure cited above).
- China's offer is unilateral and non-reciprocal, so it need not conflict with AfCFTA commitments. Its practical benefit depends on SPS/standards and on African exports beyond raw materials, an area not covered by retrieved sources (inference).

- No retrieved source on Turkey or on Gulf investment beyond the AU-UAE joint-statement headline.
