## 1. When and how was AfCFTA created, who drove it, and what is the sequence of milestones?

### Takeaway
AfCFTA is the culmination of a pan-African integration vision traceable to the 1980 Lagos Plan of Action and the 1991 Abuja Treaty (African Economic Community). The AU decided in 2012 to create a continental FTA; the Agreement was signed in Kigali on 21 March 2018 (44 signatories), entered into force on 30 May 2019, was operationally launched at the Niger summit in 2019, and preferential trading formally began on 1 January 2021.

### Cited Findings
- The CFTA is described as the culmination of a vision set out in the Lagos Plan of Action (adopted by Heads of State in 1980), which led to the Abuja Treaty establishing the African Economic Community in 1991; the Abuja Treaty entered into force on 12 May 1994 with a gradual, stage-based approach to the African Economic Community — [Globalex (NYU Law)](https://www.nyulawglobal.org/globalex/afcfta.html); see also [tralac, Comparing the Abuja Treaty and the AfCFTA Agreement](https://www.tralac.org/blog/article/16331-comparing-the-abuja-treaty-and-the-afcfta-agreement.html)
- In 2012 the AU decided to establish a continental FTA to which all 55 AU Member States would belong, which became known as the AfCFTA — [Globalex (NYU Law)](https://www.nyulawglobal.org/globalex/afcfta.html)
- On 21 March 2018, leaders met in Kigali, Rwanda (10th Extraordinary Session of the AU Assembly) to sign the AfCFTA; 44 of the 55 AU Member States signed on that occasion — [tralac, 10th Extraordinary Session of the Assembly on AfCFTA held in Kigali](https://www.tralac.org/news/article/12865-10th-extraordinary-session-of-the-assembly-of-the-african-union-on-afcfta-held-in-kigali.html); [Globalex](https://www.nyulawglobal.org/globalex/afcfta.html); the "44 signatories in Kigali" figure is repeated in [Nairametrics, 8 Sept 2026, "AfCFTA at five"](https://nairametrics.com/2026/09/08/afcfta-at-five-the-3-4-trillion-market-africa-still-wont-trade-in/)
- The Dispute Settlement Protocol text carries a 21 March 2018 date, i.e. the Framework Agreement and Protocols on Goods, Services and Dispute Settlement were adopted together at Kigali — [AfricanLII, Protocol on Rules and Procedures on the Settlement of Disputes (2018-03-21)](https://africanlii.org/en/akn/aa-au/act/protocol/2018/rules-and-procedures-on-the-settlement-of-disputes/eng@2018-03-21/publication)
- Entry into force: 30 May 2019, thirty days after the 22nd instrument of ratification was received on 29 April 2019 (the Agreement's threshold was 22 ratifications) — [Globalex](https://www.nyulawglobal.org/globalex/afcfta.html); [tralac, The AfCFTA: what has been achieved and when will it become operational?](https://www.tralac.org/publications/article/13134-the-afcfta-what-has-been-achieved-and-when-will-it-become-operational.html)
- The "operational phase" of the AfCFTA was launched at the Niger Summit of the AU (Niamey, 2019) — [tralac, Operational phase of the AfCFTA launched at Niger Summit of the African Union](https://www.tralac.org/news/article/14142-operational-phase-of-the-african-continental-free-trade-area-launched-at-niger-summit-of-the-african-union.html)
- Actual trading under AfCFTA preferences began on 1 January 2021 — [Nairametrics, 8 Sept 2026](https://nairametrics.com/2026/09/08/afcfta-at-five-the-3-4-trillion-market-africa-still-wont-trade-in/)
- Governance/leadership: Wamkele Mene (South Africa) is the first Secretary-General of the AfCFTA Secretariat, in post since 2020, and was reappointed for a further four-year term by the AU Assembly at its 37th Ordinary Session in Addis Ababa (February 2024); his first term included the Guided Trade Initiative and the launch of the Pan-African Payment and Settlement System (PAPSS) — [Ghana News Agency, Feb 2024](https://gna.org.gh/2024/02/mene-gets-the-nod-for-a-second-term-as-afcfta-secretary-general/); [TradeMark Africa](https://trademarkafrica.com/news/au-reappoints-wamkele-mene-as-afcfta-secretary-general)
- The 18th AfCFTA Council of Ministers (Abuja, around end-June/early July 2026) framed the Agreement's legal architecture as "now substantially in place", with emphasis shifting "from negotiating the rules of African trade to making them work" — [AfCFTA Secretariat press release, July 2026](https://au-afcfta.org/2026/07/afcfta-council-of-ministers-concludes-18th-meeting-in-abuja-nigeria-assumes-the-chairpersonship-as-focus-shifts-fully-to-implementation/)

### Inferences
- The milestone chain (1980 Lagos Plan -> 1991 Abuja Treaty -> 2012 Assembly decision -> 2018 Kigali signature -> 2019 entry into force and operational launch -> 2021 trading start) takes roughly 41 years from the Lagos Plan, about 30 years from Abuja, and about 9 years from the 2012 decision to first trading. The 2018-2021 interval shows that "entry into force" (May 2019) and "trading" (Jan 2021) are distinct: the originally planned 1 July 2020 trading start slipped to 1 Jan 2021 (the slip is known to me from background; I could not verify the COVID-19 cause in this session).
- The 5-year-anniversary framing used by Nairametrics (Sept 2026, "AfCFTA at five") counts from the 1 Jan 2021 trading start, not from signature or entry into force.

### Gaps
- Not verified with a retrievable URL in this session (background knowledge only, treat as UNVERIFIED until checked against au.int / tralac): the AU Agenda 2063 link (CFTA is a flagship project of Agenda 2063's First Ten-Year Implementation Plan); the January 2012 Assembly decision with an original 2017 target date; the June 2015 launch of negotiations at the AU Summit in Johannesburg; Mahamadou Issoufou (Niger) as AU "Champion" of the CFTA; Nigeria and Benin signing at the 7 July 2019 Niamey summit (leaving Eritrea as the sole non-signatory); the Secretariat seat in Accra, Ghana (decided at Niamey 2019); Mene taking office in March 2020 after appointment at the February 2020 Assembly; the Kwame Nkrumah-era pan-Africanist lineage and the 1963 OAU context. One search result (Ghana-related items such as the Ghana News Agency and "Ghana AfCFTA December 2025 update" on trade.gov) is consistent with the Accra seat, but no page I could read states it explicitly.
- The exact set of 22 ratifiers on 29 April 2019 and the exact date of the Niamey launch were not retrievable.

## 2. What exactly does the agreement commit state parties to, and what do "Phase I/II/III" mean?

### Takeaway
The AfCFTA is a Framework Agreement plus Protocols and Annexes. Phase I (in force) is Trade in Goods, Trade in Services and Dispute Settlement; Phase II covers Investment, Competition Policy and Intellectual Property Rights (adopted Feb 2023), plus Digital Trade and Women and Youth in Trade (adopted Feb 2024; many sources group these under Phase II, others call the digital protocol "Phase III"). The Phase II/III protocols are adopted but not yet in force: each needs 22 ratifications plus 30 days. On goods, State Parties commit to liberalize 90% of tariff lines (non-LDCs over 5 years, LDCs over 10), with up to 7% sensitive (10/13 years) and up to 3% excluded; SACU and a "G6" group have special arrangements.

### Cited Findings
**Structure and phases**
- The AfCFTA's Phase I covers the Protocols on Trade in Goods and Trade in Services and the Protocol on Rules and Procedures on the Settlement of Disputes (all signed at Kigali, March 2018) — [AfricanLII (Dispute Settlement Protocol)](https://africanlii.org/en/akn/aa-au/act/protocol/2018/rules-and-procedures-on-the-settlement-of-disputes/eng@2018-03-21/publication); [tralac, AfCFTA Phase II and III negotiations update](https://www.tralac.org/blog/article/15090-afcfta-phase-ii-and-iii-negotiations-update.html)
- Phase II negotiations on Investment, Competition Policy and Intellectual Property Rights were concluded in October 2022 and the three Protocols were adopted by the AU Assembly at its 36th Ordinary Session (18-19 February 2023, Addis Ababa) — [tralac, AfCFTA FAQs](https://www.tralac.org/publications/article/13784-afcfta-questions-and-answers.html); [UCT IP Chair, The AfCFTA Protocol on Intellectual Property Rights](https://law.uct.ac.za/ip-chair/articles/2021-07-17-afcfta-protocol-intellectual-property-rights-what-when-how-and-why)
- The Protocol on Intellectual Property Rights has a Preamble, 7 Parts and 42 Articles — [UCT IP Chair](https://law.uct.ac.za/ip-chair/articles/2021-07-17-afcfta-protocol-intellectual-property-rights-what-when-how-and-why)
- The Protocol on Digital Trade was adopted at the 37th Ordinary Session of the AU Assembly, 17-18 February 2024, Addis Ababa; it covers electronic transactions, data protection and privacy, consumer rights, cybersecurity, digital inclusion and emerging technologies; its annexes address digital identities, cross-border digital payments, cross-border data transfers, online safety, source-code disclosure exceptions, emerging technologies (including AI) and fintech — [Covington, Adoption of the AfCFTA Protocol on Digital Trade](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2024/02/africa-technology-regulatory-update-adoption-of-the-afcfta-protocol-on-digital-trade); [IISD, The AfCFTA Digital Protocol](https://www.iisd.org/articles/policy-analysis/afcfta-digital-protocol)
- The Protocol on Women and Youth in Trade was released in February 2024 and is described as the "eighth and final" AfCFTA protocol (the eight being Goods, Services, Dispute Settlement, Investment, Competition, IPR, Digital Trade, Women and Youth) — [UNECA blog, The AfCFTA protocol on women and youth in trade](https://www.uneca.org/stories/blog-the-afcfta-protocol-on-women-and-youth-in-trade-%E2%80%93-africa%E2%80%99s-blueprint-for-inclusive)
- Terminology varies by source: tralac's FAQ lists Digital Trade and Women and Youth as "Phase II" instruments adopted in Feb 2024, while the brief given to me (and other commentary) calls Digital Trade "Phase III" — [tralac FAQ (May 2025)](https://www.tralac.org/documents/resources/faqs/5889-afcfta-frequently-asked-questions-updated-may-2025/file.html)
- Entry-into-force rule for the Phase II protocols (Investment, Competition, IPR, Digital Trade, Women and Youth): 30 days after deposit of the 22nd instrument of ratification — [tralac FAQ (May 2025)](https://www.tralac.org/documents/resources/faqs/5889-afcfta-frequently-asked-questions-updated-may-2025/file.html); [tralac, Must the Phase II AfCFTA Protocols be ratified to be implemented?](https://www.tralac.org/publications/article/17080-must-the-phase-ii-afcfta-protocols-be-ratified-to-be-implemented.html)
- As of the 18th Council of Ministers (Abuja, ~early July 2026) "a limited number of legal instruments remain under negotiation", notably an outstanding Annex to the Investment Protocol and a remaining Annex to the Protocol on Intellectual Property Rights — [AfCFTA Secretariat, July 2026](https://au-afcfta.org/2026/07/afcfta-council-of-ministers-concludes-18th-meeting-in-abuja-nigeria-assumes-the-chairpersonship-as-focus-shifts-fully-to-implementation/)
- In February 2026 the AU Assembly adopted the eight Annexes to the Protocol on Intellectual Property Rights; South Africa has concluded its domestic processes to ratify the Protocol on Competition Policy and the Protocol on Women and Youth in Trade — [dtic (South Africa) AfCFTA Newsletter, March 2026](https://www.thedtic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/Newsletter-on-the-AfCFTA-March-2026.pdf) (surfaced via search; see also [amcham.co.za copy](https://www.amcham.co.za/sites/default/files/content-files/Newsletter%20on%20the%20AfCFTA%20-%20March%202026.pdf))
- South Africa formally ratified the Protocol on Women and Youth in Trade as of June 2025 — [APA News](https://apanews.net/south-africa-ratifies-afcfta-protocol-on-women-and-youth-in-trade/)
- Academic commentary notes the Phase II protocols were negotiated and adopted but not yet in force, with some annexes still under negotiation and legislatures needing to approve once annexes are done — [tralac, The scope of the AfCFTA is expanding](https://www.tralac.org/blog/article/15909-the-scope-of-the-afcfta-is-expanding-what-are-the-implications.html); [Harvard ILJ, The AfCFTA Investment Protocol (2025)](https://journals.law.harvard.edu/ilj/2025/04/the-afcfta-investment-protocol-a-new-age-for-regional-investment-and-dispute-resolution/)

**Dispute settlement**
- The Dispute Settlement Mechanism is administered by a Dispute Settlement Body (DSB) comprising all State Parties, modelled on the WTO Dispute Settlement Understanding; the DSB establishes panels (3 members for two disputing parties, 5 if more) and a standing Appellate Body of 7 persons (3 sit on any one case, 4-year terms renewable once) — [Mondaq, Mauritius and the AfCFTA Part 3: Dispute Settlement](https://www.mondaq.com/international-trade-investment/1373790/mauritius-and-the-afcfta-part-3-dispute-settlement); [AfCFTA Secretariat, Dispute Settlement Mechanism](https://au-afcfta.org/?p=1932)

**Trade in services**
- Five priority services sectors: financial, communications, transport, tourism and business services — [AfCFTA Secretariat, Trade in Services](https://au-afcfta.org/trade-in-services)
- 49 Member States have submitted initial services offers in the five priority sectors; 24 draft Schedules of Specific Commitments have been adopted for provisional implementation (figures as given in a search summary; date of the underlying statement not confirmed, one item says "as of February 2025, 24 State Parties") — [AfCFTA Secretariat, Trade in Services](https://au-afcfta.org/trade-in-services); [trade.gov, Ghana AfCFTA services negotiations update](https://www.trade.gov/market-intelligence/ghana-afcfta-services-negotiations-update)
- Nigeria's stakeholders validated a draft services offer in Abuja (June 2026) and Nigeria submitted its services schedule to ECOWAS in October 2025 — [Gazette NGR, June 2026](https://gazettengr.com/stakeholders-validate-nigerias-draft-offer-for-afcfta-trade-protocol/)

**Tariff liberalization commitments (Protocol on Trade in Goods modalities)**
- All State Parties fully liberalize 90% of tariff lines (non-sensitive): non-LDCs over 5 years, LDCs over 10 years — [South Centre PB63, Phase 1B of AfCFTA negotiations](https://www.southcentre.int/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/PB63_Phase-1B-of-the-AfCFTA-negotiations_EN-1.pdf); [UNCTAD, Designing trade liberalization in Africa: modalities for tariff negotiations](https://unctad.org/system/files/official-document/ditctncd2019d4_en.pdf)
- Sensitive products: up to 7% of tariff lines, liberalized over 10 years (non-LDC) or 13 years (LDC) — same sources; [UN LDC Portal (tralac's Trudi Hartzenberg, June 2024)](https://www.un.org/ldcportal/content/african-continental-free-trade-area-agreement-what-expected-ldcs-terms-trade-liberalisation)
- Excluded list: up to 3% of tariff lines, subject to a cap that excluded products may not exceed 10% of a Party's total intra-African import value — same sources
- SACU: 5-year phase-down for the SACU common external tariff, unless a customs union with both LDC and non-LDC members commits to a 10-year phase-down, in which case the SACU CET is also liberalized over 10 years — [UN LDC Portal](https://www.un.org/ldcportal/content/african-continental-free-trade-area-agreement-what-expected-ldcs-terms-trade-liberalisation); [South Centre PB63](https://www.southcentre.int/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/PB63_Phase-1B-of-the-AfCFTA-negotiations_EN-1.pdf)
- "G6" special regime (Ethiopia, Madagascar, Malawi, Sudan, Zambia, Zimbabwe): 85% of tariff lines liberalized over 10 years (linear cuts) plus a further 5% over 15 years; how the remaining 10% is split between sensitive and excluded lines is left to negotiation. One search summary mentions a "Group of Seven" that adds Djibouti; I could not confirm that. — [UNECA, Brief assessment of AfCFTA modalities (Nov 2018)](https://archive.uneca.org/sites/default/files/PublicationFiles/brief_assessment_of_afcfta_modalities_eng_nov18.pdf); [UN LDC Portal](https://www.un.org/ldcportal/node/1272); [Zimbabwe Independent / Newsday on Zimbabwe](https://newsday.co.zw/local-news/article/200002314/zim-steps-up-afcfta-efforts)
- Sensitive-list (Category B) tariff reductions were reported to begin on 1 January 2026 (i.e. after the initial grace period), with Categories A and B together covering 97% of tariff lines — [Malawi Nation, "Tariff delays stall Malawi's AfCFTA trade"](https://mwnation.com/tariff-delays-stall-mws-afcfta-trade/)
- Preferential trade requires a State Party to gazette its provisional schedule of tariff concessions and have agreed rules of origin; as of January 2026, 24 States (including Malawi) had gazetted schedules — [Malawi Nation](https://mwnation.com/tariff-delays-stall-mws-afcfta-trade/); in Sept 2026 sources still say 24 (Rio Times, below) with Nigeria (gazetted ECOWAS schedule, reflected in South Africa's customs notice 12 Sept 2025) and Ethiopia (24 Oct 2025) among recent gazetters — [dtic Newsletter, March 2026](https://www.thedtic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/Newsletter-on-the-AfCFTA-March-2026.pdf)
- Rules of origin: conflicting reports on completeness. The dtic March 2026 newsletter says the outstanding automotive and clothing/textile rules were adopted, i.e. 100% agreed — [dtic, March 2026](https://www.thedtic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/Newsletter-on-the-AfCFTA-March-2026.pdf); a Sept 2026 summary of the Rio Times says 92.4% of rules of origin are finalized — [Rio Times, AfCFTA status 2026](https://www.riotimesonline.com/afcfta-status-2026/); AllBusiness.africa says 92.3% of tariff lines are covered, with textiles, clothing and automotive outstanding and finalisation expected by Feb 2026 — [AllBusiness.africa, AfCFTA Implementation 2026](https://allbusiness.africa/insights/afcfta-implementation-2026)
- Guided Trade Initiative (GTI): expanded from 7 to 39 participating countries; its conclusion was confirmed at the 16th Council of Ministers (April 2025) — [AllBusiness.africa](https://allbusiness.africa/insights/afcfta-implementation-2026) (secondary source)
- More than 12,000 certificates of origin issued under the AfCFTA and notified to the Secretariat as of March 2026; a gradual shift toward manufactured and agri-food products — [AfCFTA Secretariat, July 2026](https://au-afcfta.org/2026/07/afcfta-council-of-ministers-concludes-18th-meeting-in-abuja-nigeria-assumes-the-chairpersonship-as-focus-shifts-fully-to-implementation/)

### Inferences
- With trading starting 1 Jan 2021, the 5-year non-LDC phase-down for non-sensitive goods should have finished around 2025-2026 for States whose schedules were gazetted on time, and the sensitive-list (10-year, after a grace period) cuts only start in 2026; the practical effect is that only about 24 State Parties (of 50 ratifiers) can currently apply preferences, so legal commitments run ahead of practice. The January 2026 start of Category B reductions is a single-source item (Malawi Nation) and should be checked against the Protocol on Trade in Goods annexes.
- The 90/7/3 split gives a ceiling of 97% liberalization (non-sensitive plus sensitive) before the excluded 3%, but the 10%-of-import-value cap on exclusions and the G6 85/5/10 arrangement mean effective coverage differs by country.

### Gaps
- Number of ratifications for each Phase II/III protocol (Investment, Competition, IPR, Digital Trade, Women and Youth) as of October 2026: no source found giving a count; the evidence (outstanding annexes in July 2026; only South Africa's ratification news surfaced) suggests none has reached the 22-instrument threshold, but this is not confirmed. The tralac status tracker and AU treaty-status list could not be accessed.
- Whether the Digital Trade Protocol annexes were all adopted (Feb 2025 or later) and whether any Phase II protocol has entered into force.
- Operational status of the Dispute Settlement Body (panel roster, Appellate Body appointments): not found.
- Verified text of Annex 1 modalities on LDC/SACU/G6 from the primary legal text (only secondary summaries retrieved); whether "Zimbabwe" and "Djibouti" are in the special group per final schedules.
- Status of Annex on Rules of Origin: 92.3-92.4% vs 100% conflict is unresolved; the most recent official statement (the 18th Council of Ministers press release) was not read in full.

## 3. How many countries have signed and ratified; who leads the Secretariat and where is it based?

### Takeaway
54 of the 55 AU Member States have signed (Eritrea is the sole non-signatory). As of September 2026, 50 signatories have deposited ratification instruments: Somalia became the 50th State Party in early September 2026. Benin, Libya, South Sudan and Sudan have signed but not yet completed ratification. The Secretariat is headed by Secretary-General Wamkele Mene (reappointed Feb 2024 for four years).

### Cited Findings
- As at end-September 2026, 50 of the 54 signatories had deposited their instruments of ratification with the AU Commission Chairperson (the depositary); Benin, Libya, South Sudan and Sudan are yet to complete ratification; Eritrea is the only AU Member State not to have signed — [Rio Times, AfCFTA status 2026](https://www.riotimesonline.com/afcfta-status-2026/); [Nairametrics, 8 Sept 2026](https://nairametrics.com/2026/09/08/afcfta-at-five-the-3-4-trillion-market-africa-still-wont-trade-in/); [tralac, Status of AfCFTA Ratification (infographic)](https://www.tralac.org/resources/infographic/13795-status-of-afcfta-ratification.html)
- Somalia deposited its instrument of ratification at the AU headquarters in Addis Ababa and became the 50th State Party; Secretary-General Mene then visited Mogadishu (August/September 2026 coverage) — [Dawan Africa](https://www.dawan.africa/news/somalia-ratifies-afcfta-becomes-50th-member-and-eyes-made-in-somalia-exports); [AfCFTA Secretariat, "From ratification to the first shipment: the AfCFTA Secretary-General in Mogadishu"](https://au-afcfta.org/2026/08/from-ratification-to-the-first-shipment-the-afcfta-secretary-general-in-mogadishu/). The deposit date is reported inconsistently: 2 September 2026 in one search summary (Rio Times) vs 4 September 2026 in another (Dawan Africa-based summary).
- Somalia's parliament had approved ratification earlier (October 2025 coverage), meaning there was roughly an 11-month gap between parliamentary approval and deposit — [African Newspage, Oct 2025](https://www.africannewspage.net/2025/10/somali-parliament-ratifies-afcfta-agreement/)

- At Kigali (March 2018) 44 States signed; ratification threshold for entry into force was 22 (reached 29 April 2019) — [Globalex](https://www.nyulawglobal.org/globalex/afcfta.html)
- Nairametrics (8 Sept 2026) says Somalia had "approved but not yet completed the paperwork", which conflicts with the Rio Times/Dawan reports that Somalia had already deposited by 2-4 September; likely a timing artefact since 50 = 54 minus the four named non-ratifiers — [Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2026/09/08/afcfta-at-five-the-3-4-trillion-market-africa-still-wont-trade-in/); [Rio Times](https://www.riotimesonline.com/afcfta-status-2026/)

- Secretariat leadership: Wamkele Mene, first Secretary-General (since 2020), reappointed Feb 2024 for another four-year term (so through about early 2028) — [Ghana News Agency](https://gna.org.gh/2024/02/mene-gets-the-nod-for-a-second-term-as-afcfta-secretary-general/); [TradeMark Africa](https://trademarkafrica.com/news/au-reappoints-wamkele-mene-as-afcfta-secretary-general)

### Inferences
- Roughly 93% of signatories (50 of 54) and 91% of AU members (50 of 55) are now State Parties; the remaining ratification gap is four states, three of them conflict-affected (Libya, South Sudan, Sudan), plus Benin and the non-signatory Eritrea. ## 4. What are the best available economic indicators and how credible are the projections?

### Takeaway
Intra-African trade is roughly US$214 billion (2025, Afreximbank), about 14-16% of Africa's total trade, versus roughly 58-66% intra-regional export shares for Asia and Europe (UNCTAD). The most cited gain estimate is the World Bank (2020): +7% real income (about US$450 billion) by 2035, 30 million people out of extreme poverty, wage gains around 10%, but about two-thirds of the gain depends on trade facilitation, not tariffs. UNECA gives a range of headline numbers (52.3% from tariff removal in a 2012 paper; 45% intra-African trade growth and +1.2% GDP by 2045 in 2025 modeling).

### Cited Findings
**Intra-African trade values**

### Inferences
- Intra-African trade share depends on definition: UNCTAD's 16.9% (2024) is the share of exports going to Africa; Afreximbank's ~US$214 billion over about US$1.5 trillion total merchandise trade gives about 14% by simple division (my calculation), consistent with the "about 15-16%" range quoted. Either way the share is at the 2015 peak area (18.8%) or below and the AfCFTA has not yet produced a visible structural jump (as Nairametrics Sept 2026 also notes).
- The World Bank and ECA numbers are long-horizon CGE (computable general equilibrium) projections, 2035 and 2045 respectively, contingent on full tariff phase-downs, trade facilitation and removal of non-tariff barriers; since about two-thirds of the World Bank gain is from facilitation and since the actual implementation (24 gazetted schedules, delayed services and Phase II protocols) lags, the 2035 gains should be considered an upper-bound scenario, not a forecast. The 52.3% figure is an older, tariff-only partial estimate and should not be presented alongside the World Bank figure as an equivalent.
- The 2024 value revision (US$220.3 billion to US$202.7 billion) shows that headline growth rates (+12.4% in 2024, +5.5% in 2025) are sensitive to data revisions; a writer should cite the edition and year of each figure.

## 5. What is the latest news (2025-2026) on status, protocols, Assembly decisions and Council outcomes?

### Takeaway
2026 has been framed as the move from negotiation to implementation: the 39th AU Assembly (14-15 Feb 2026) commended progress, adopted the eight IPR Protocol annexes and launched AfCFTA digital identity and traceability systems; the 18th Council of Ministers (Abuja, ~early July 2026) passed the chair from Egypt to Nigeria and declared the legal architecture substantially in place; Somalia became the 50th State Party in early September 2026; Afreximbank reported 2025 intra-African trade of US$213.8 billion (+5.5%). Outstanding issues: schedule gazettal (about 24 states), two remaining annexes (Investment, IPR), and Phase II ratifications.
