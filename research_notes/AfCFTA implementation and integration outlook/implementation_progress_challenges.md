## 1. What has actually been implemented versus merely adopted on paper, and how many countries trade under AfCFTA preferences in practice?

### Takeaway
By early 2026 the legal architecture for goods is largely adopted (about 49 of 54 AU members ratified, about 48 tariff offers verified, about 92% of rules-of-origin lines agreed, and automotive/textile transitional rules signed off in Sept 2025 and Feb 2026). Actual preferential trading is far narrower. Only about 24-25 countries (SACU counted as a bloc or as five countries, depending on the source) had gazetted schedules and started preferential trade by early 2026, and certificate-of-origin volumes are small and concentrated in South Africa and Egypt. Phase II protocols (Investment, Competition, IPR, Digital Trade, Women and Youth) are adopted but not demonstrably in force.

### Cited Findings

**Ratification and tariff schedules**
- As of January 2026, 49 of 54 AU members (about 90%) had deposited ratification instruments — [Habari Network, Jan 2026](https://www.thehabarinetwork.com/afcfta-january-2026-update-progress-and-tough-road-ahead) (secondary; not verified against the AU Commission).
- 50 tariff offers on goods had been submitted (individually or by customs unions CEMAC, EAC, ECOWAS, SACU), and 48 verified by the Secretariat as conforming to the modalities. Libya, Sudan, Eritrea, Somalia, Djibouti and Sahrawi Republic had not submitted — [Rio Times, "AfCFTA status 2026"](https://www.riotimesonline.com/afcfta-status-2026/); the "48 verified" figure is dated to May 2025 in [tralac, July 2025 progress report](https://www.tralac.org/documents/resources/cfta/7851-progress-report-of-the-african-continental-free-trade-area-july-2025/file.html) / [tralac 2025 conference two-pager](https://www.tralac.org/documents/events/tralac/5877-2025-conference-two-pager-afcfta-update-trade-in-goods-negotiations/file.html).
- Only 17 states had filed complete schedules covering Categories A, B and C as of July 2025 — [tralac, July 2025 progress report](https://www.tralac.org/documents/resources/cfta/7851-progress-report-of-the-african-continental-free-trade-area-july-2025/file.html).
- Liberalisation modalities: 90% of tariff lines liberalised over 5 years (non-LDCs) or 10 years (LDCs); 7% sensitive (13 years for LDCs, 10 for non-LDCs); 3% excluded (not more than 10% of import value). A "G6" (Ethiopia, Madagascar, Malawi, Sudan, Zambia plus Zimbabwe) secured 15-year phase-downs — [WITA](https://www.wita.org/?p=19543), [tralac](https://www.tralac.org/blog/article/15584.html), [Bowmans](https://bowmanslaw.com/?p=51118).
- Phase-down of Category B tariffs was expected to start 1 January 2026 — [tralac Kenya factsheet](https://www.tralac.org/documents/resources/factsheets/5714-factsheet-trading-under-the-afcfta-kenya.html) (via search summary; date of the factsheet unknown).

**Countries actually trading under preferences**

- DISCREPANCY: counts range from 23 (earlier tralac), to 24 (Ethiopia's Oct 2025 regulation refers to 24 member states), to 25 (March 2026, counting SACU). The named list contains 24 and includes all five SACU members individually, so "25" may count SACU as an additional unit or a recently added state. Treat "about 24-25 of 54" as the best estimate — [Capital Ethiopia, Nov 2025](https://capitalethiopia.com/2025/11/09/ethiopia-implements-customs-tariff-reductions-with-24-afcfta-member-states-to-boost-continental-trade/).
- Country milestones: South Africa gazetted SACU's schedule 26 Jan 2024 and launched preferential trade 31 Jan 2024 — [SARS](https://www.sars.gov.za/legal-lsec-ce-ta-2024-016-sch-1-1-1892-substitution-general-note-o-to-implement-the-afcfta-agreement-26-january-2024). Kenya and Egypt are reported as implementing their provisional schedules on 31 Aug 2023 — [tralac Kenya factsheet](https://www.tralac.org/documents/resources/factsheets/5714-factsheet-trading-under-the-afcfta-kenya.html) (via search summary, not verified). Nigeria gazetted the ECOWAS Provisional Schedule in April 2025 (zero duty on about 90% of lines) — [AllAfrica, Apr 2025](https://allafrica.com/stories/202504150096.html) — and was added to South Africa's Schedule 1 General Note O on 12 Sept 2025 — [Gazette NG](https://gazettengr.com/fg-gazettes-ecowas-tariffs-advancing-afcfta-implementation/); a Nigerian preferential-tariff rollout was reported Nov 2025 — [AllAfrica, 18 Nov 2025](https://allafrica.com/stories/202511180056.html). Ethiopia implemented via Council of Ministers Regulation 574/2025, with cuts beginning 9 Oct 2025 against 24 partners — [Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/ethiopia-implements-afcfta-tariff-concessions-council-of-ministers-regulation-no-574-2025), [FreshPlaza](https://www.freshplaza.com/africa/article/9783782/ethiopia-begins-duty-cuts-under-afcfta-trade-deal/).
- Bilateral coverage is partial: Malawi can trade preferentially with only about half of AfCFTA members because others have not concluded tariff concessions or rules of origin — [Malawi Nation](https://mwnation.com/tariff-delays-stall-mws-afcfta-trade/).
- South Africa's top export destinations under AfCFTA preferences: Ghana, Kenya, Egypt, Rwanda, Morocco — [Rio Times](https://www.riotimesonline.com/afcfta-status-2026/).

**Rules of origin (RoO)**
- RoO negotiated for about 92.43% of tariff lines by 2025, with the unresolved lines concentrated in textiles/clothing and automotive — [tralac July 2025 progress report](https://www.tralac.org/documents/resources/cfta/7851-progress-report-of-the-african-continental-free-trade-area-july-2025/file.html); [Financial Afrik, Sept 2025](https://www.financialafrik.com/en/2025/09/06/afcfta-rules-of-origin-facing-obstacles-in-the-automotive-and-textile-sectors/).
- The 17th Council of Trade Ministers (Cairo, Sept 2025) agreed transitional RoO for textiles/apparel and automotive: vehicles and components (HS 8701-8716) need at least 40% African content, up to 60% non-originating inputs allowed, subject to review after five years as an interim measure. The 39th AU Assembly (Feb 2026) formally signed off the automotive RoO — [Engineering News, 6 Mar 2026](https://engineeringnews.co.za/article/major-step-forward-as-african-union-signs-off-on-made-in-africa-auto-trade-rules-aaam-2026-03-06); [Amwal Al Ghad](https://en.amwalalghad.com/?p=218407); [Arab Finance](https://arabfinance.com/en/news/newdetails/Egypt-secures-African-consensus-on-outstanding-AfCFTA-rules-of-origin). A roadmap for completing the rules after the transitional phase was also developed.
- Certificates of origin: 8,561 as of June 2025; "more than 10,000" and "more than 12,000" reported for end-March 2026 (DISCREPANCY between the two 2026 figures, likely different counting dates or sources); South Africa 4,658, Egypt 2,852 (date unknown); Tanzania 392 by June 2025 — [AllBusiness Africa](https://allbusiness.africa/insights/afcfta-implementation-2026); [The EastAfrican](https://theeastafrican.co.ke/tea/business-tech/tanzania-ahead-of-peers-in-trading-under-afcfta-5524096); [AmCham SA newsletter](https://www.amcham.co.za/sites/default/files/content-files/Newsletter%20on%20the%20AfCFTA%20-%20March%202026.pdf). Earlier series: 13 certificates in 2022 rising to over 2,600 (date unknown, about 2024) — [Africa Renewal](https://africarenewal.un.org/en/magazine/afcftas-guided-trade-initiative-takes-set-ease-and-boost-intra-african-trade).

**Guided Trade Initiative (GTI)**
- Unveiled 25-26 July 2022 with seven countries (Rwanda, Cameroon, Egypt, Ghana, Kenya, Mauritius, Tanzania); formally launched in Accra on 7 Oct 2022 with eight State Parties (Tunisia joined) and 96 products for duty-free, quota-free trade, with plans to double or triple in 2023 — [trade.gov GTI page](https://www.trade.gov/market-intelligence/african-continental-free-trade-area-afcfta-guided-trade-initiative); [Africa Renewal](https://africarenewal.un.org/en/magazine/afcftas-guided-trade-initiative-takes-set-ease-and-boost-intra-african-trade).
- Pilot scale: over 100 "potential trades" in total; Ghana 14 companies / about 40 trades, Kenya 27 companies / over 50 trades, Egypt 7 companies / 20 trades, Mauritius 25 potential trades, Rwanda 8, Cameroon 4. Kenya's first shipment was Exide batteries worth US$77,000 to Ghana, followed by tea — [BFT Online](https://thebftonline.com/?p=127323); [AU-AfCFTA](https://au-afcfta.org/?p=11012) (search-summary level).
- Products named: batteries, tea, coffee, ceramic tiles, processed meat, corn starch, sugar, pasta, glucose syrup, dried fruit, sisal fibre, electronic panels, air-conditioning components — same sources.
- Second phase announced 2023 to widen products and participants; GTI participation reported at 39 countries as of Sept 2024 (the search tool did not name the source for this figure; treat as unverified) — [trade.gov](https://www.trade.gov/market-intelligence/african-continental-free-trade-area-afcfta-guided-trade-initiative); [Gazette NG](https://gazettengr.com/chiamaka-adinnu-how-guided-trade-initiative-is-boosting-african-continental-free-trade-agreement/). South Africa joined the GTI — [Trade Unions in AfCFTA](https://tradeunionsinafcfta.org/south-africa-joins-the-afcfta-guided-trade-initiative/).
- Reported GTI obstacles: NTBs, complex RoO, poor infrastructure, limited internet access, instability and SME finance — [BFT Online](https://thebftonline.com/?p=127323); [ODI](https://odi.org/en/insights/the-afcfta-marks-a-milestone-in-its-implementation/).

### Inferences
- "Adopted" far exceeds "operational". Roughly 24-25 of 54 states can trade on preferences. Many bilateral pairs remain unusable because a partner has not gazetted or because RoO were unfinished until 2025-26 (Malawi example), so the effective share of country pairs with live preferences is much lower than 45%.
- Certificate counts (about 12,000 by March 2026 over about 3 years) are not value-weighted. No source gave the share of intra-African trade (about US$214bn) actually moving under AfCFTA preferences, so preference utilisation is unmeasured in the material I found. If the South Africa and Egypt figures refer to the same date, the two together are about 60% of the 12,000 total (my arithmetic, dates uncertain), which would imply concentration among the larger economies.
- Automotive/textile RoO were resolved only in Sept 2025-Feb 2026, so these sectors have had almost no time to generate preferential trade; any trade effect through 2025 cannot reflect them.
- PAPSS volume +1,000% against value +120% implies the average ticket fell by roughly 80% (value multiplier about 2.2 divided by volume multiplier about 11; my arithmetic). This is consistent with growth from a very small base into small payments. Absolute levels are needed before drawing conclusions.
- The GTI was a pilot by design (about 100 trades at launch), and its critique of "small scale" is supported by the company/trade counts above; its main value was as a test of procedures and as a signal.

## 2. What does the evidence say about intra-African trade trends 2019-2025 (including pandemic distortions and the 2022-2025 commodity/price cycle)?

### Takeaway
Intra-African trade is about US$200-220 billion and roughly 15% of Africa's total trade (about 15-18% by a looser reading), with no clear structural break since AfCFTA trading began on 1 Jan 2021. Year-to-year movements track the commodity/price cycle, a few large traders and statistical revisions more than preferential-tariff effects. The 2024 headline was revised down by about US$18 billion between Afreximbank's 2025 and 2026 reports.

### Cited Findings
- Afreximbank African Trade Report 2025 (launched June 2025, Abuja): intra-African trade +12.4% to US$220.3 billion in 2024, after a 5.9% contraction in 2023; forecast average growth of 6.6% a year for 2025-2029 — [Bizcommunity](https://www.bizcommunity.com/article/afreximbank-launches-african-trade-report-2025-259357a); [Voice of Nigeria](https://von.gov.ng/afreximbank-unveils-2025-report-on-african-trade/); [Afreximbank ATR 2025 PDF](https://media.afreximbank.com/afrexim/African-Trade-Report_2025.pdf) (PDF not opened).
- Afreximbank African Trade Report 2026 (June 2026, theme "Leveraging Geopolitics for Trade and Industrialisation in Global Africa"): intra-African trade +5.47% to US$213.8 billion in 2025 from US$202.7 billion in 2024 (about 15% of about US$1.47 trillion total trade); Africa's merchandise trade +6.1% to about US$1.5 trillion; real GDP growth 3.4% (2024) to 4.5% (2025); inflation 21.6% to 13.1% — [Nairametrics, 25 Jun 2026](https://nairametrics.com/2026/06/25/afreximbank-intra-african-trade-rises-5-5-to-213-8-billion/); [Arise](https://www.arise.tv/intra-african-trade-rises-5-5-to-213-8bn-in-2025-says-afreximbank/); [Guardian NG](https://guardian.ng/business-services/intra-african-trade-rises-by-5-5-as-regional-integration-gains-momentum/); [AJOT](https://www.ajot.com/news/afreximbank-africa-trade-report-shows-africa-can-turn-geopolitical-disruptions-into-long-term-growth-opportunity).

The 2025 figure of US$213.8 billion is therefore below the originally reported 2024 level. Another outlet reported 2024 growth as "up 8%" to KSh27 trillion — [Eastleigh Voice](https://eastleighvoice.co.ke/africa%20trade-african%20continental%20free%20trade%20area-regional%20trade/137172/trade-between-african-countries-up-8pc-to-sh27-trillion-in-2024-report-shows) (underlying report not identified).

- Modelled potential: UNECA simulations put full implementation at +45% intra-African trade by 2045 (about US$276 billion) — same Ecofin link; an older UNECA estimate was +52.3% from removing import duties, doubling with NTB reductions (date and exact source unknown) — [RIS newsletter](https://ris.org.in/newsletter/RIS%20Latest%20Publications/2023/stat.pdf) (search summary). World Bank: AfCFTA could lift 40 million Africans out of extreme poverty (April 2026 reporting) — [Citi Newsroom](https://citinewsroom.com/2026/04/afcfta-could-lift-40m-africans-from-extreme-poverty-world-bank/); an income gain of US$450 billion by 2035 is cited in [rpublc](https://rpublc.com/vol9-no2/economic-uncertainties-of-the-afcfta) (secondary).
- External shocks relevant to the 2025-26 outlook: AGOA lapsed 30 Sept 2025 (one-year extension reported later), US reciprocal tariffs of 10-30% hit beneficiaries, and China began zero tariffs for 53 African countries on 1 May 2026 — [Cape Times/IOL, 1 Mar 2026](https://iol.co.za/capetimes/opinion/2026-03-01-trumps-tariffs-have-gutted-agoas-dutyfree-promise/); [FPRI](https://www.fpri.org/article/2025/10/the-african-growth-and-opportunity-act-is-no-more/); [ISS, "Why the AfCFTA can't simply plug the US trade gap"](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/why-the-afcfta-can-t-simply-plug-the-us-trade-gap) (article body not read).

### Inferences
- The 2021-2025 series do not show a visible AfCFTA break: the share stayed about 13-15% under Afreximbank/UNCTAD-style measures, rising at most 1-2 points. Revisions (US$220.3bn to US$202.7bn for 2024) are larger than the 2025 growth, so "growth since 2021" cannot be stated robustly.
- Preferential tariffs only became live for most large economies between 2023 and Oct 2025 (SACU Jan 2024, Nigeria Sept-Nov 2025, Ethiopia Oct 2025), and automotive/textile RoO only in 2025-26. A 2021-2025 comparison therefore measures AfCFTA's anticipation and early implementation, not its steady-state effect.
- Visible 2025 growth is driven by identifiable commodity and firm-level stories (Nigerian refined petroleum, DRC and Zambian mineral and copper-belt flows, Ethiopian and Ugandan trade) rather than manufactured goods under preferences.
- 2020 pandemic distortions and the 2021-22 rebound cannot be quantified from the material retrieved.

### Gaps
- No 2019, 2020, 2021 intra-African trade values or shares from a single consistent source (last planned query not run due to search budget exhaustion).
- Could not open the ATR 2025/2026 PDFs or UNCTAD's Economic Development in Africa report to resolve the definitional differences or the 2024 revision.
- No causal or counterfactual study (gravity model, difference-in-differences) of AfCFTA's effect found.
- No UNCTAD or UNECA 2026 assessment text retrieved directly.

## 3. Which obstacles are most binding (World Bank, UNECA, Afreximbank, AfDB, UNCTAD, tralac, ITC)?

### Takeaway
Evidence points to behind-the-border and non-tariff costs (about 60% of trade costs per the World Bank, Aug 2026), weak firm awareness and RoO compliance costs, thin regional value chains, payments/trade-finance frictions and country-level fiscal and political constraints, rather than tariffs alone. Fiscal losses look small on average but are significant for a few LDCs.

### Cited Findings
**Behind-the-border and non-tariff barriers**
- World Bank "Integrating Africa: From Threads to Hubs" (brief dated 25 Aug 2026; press release 28 Aug 2026): about 60% of Africa's total trade costs arise behind the border (customs clearance inefficiencies, regulatory misalignment, weak logistics services, fragmented transit systems, poor infrastructure); four priorities: regional value chains, reducing trade/regulatory frictions, deepening and enforcing regional agreements, regional public goods (corridors, power markets, digital, payments); deeper services liberalisation could raise AfCFTA services trade by about 60-64% by 2035; "broad coverage of policies is not matched by binding commitments, and binding commitments are not matched by the governance machinery needed to enforce them" — [World Bank brief](https://www.worldbank.org/en/brief/2026/08/25/integrating-africa-from-threads-to-hubs); [World Bank press release](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2026/08/28/whats-next-for-africas-integration-agenda); [Nairametrics, 18 Aug 2026](https://nairametrics.com/2026/08/18/world-bank-60-of-africas-trade-costs-come-from-within-countries/) (report text not opened). Note that another Nairametrics piece (8 Sep 2026) describes NTBs as "roughly 60% of trade costs", which conflates "behind-the-border" with "non-tariff" — [Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2026/09/08/afcfta-at-five-the-3-4-trillion-market-africa-still-wont-trade-in/).
- NTB reporting data: COMESA-EAC-SADC Tripartite mechanism 2008-2025: 928 complaints, 803 resolved, 125 active (about 86.5%); AfCFTA online mechanism: over 600 NTBs reported by mid-2023, about 35% resolved; EAC Secretariat's 2025 report says average NTB resolution time rose from 76 days (2021) to 274 days (2024), versus an AfCFTA target of 60 days (simple) and 125 days (complex) — [TradeMark Africa / The Standard](https://trademarkafrica.com/non-tariff-barriers-could-hurt-east-africas-integration-gains/); [Mondaq Nigeria](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/international-trade-investment/1623526/non-tariff-barriers-under-the-african-continental-free-trade-area-challenges-and-opportunities-for-nigeria); [tradebarriers.africa](https://tradebarriers.africa/resolved_complaints). DISCREPANCY: UNCTAD cites 583 resolved cases (date unknown) while Mondaq's mid-2023 figures imply roughly 210 resolved, which suggests a newer 583 count.
- Customs/border: Lagos-Abidjan trucking can take up to 17 days over 1,080 km with up to 37 checkpoints; Lagos port dwell time rose from 20 to 22 days over 2011-2017; Northern Corridor Time Release Study was carried out in 2016 (EAC) — [Leadership NG](https://leadership.ng/cargo-spends-17-days-on-lagos-abidjan-route-amid-37-checkpoints/); [NAN](https://nannews.ng/?p=327072); [NCTTCA TRS](https://ttcanc.org/sites/default/files/2023-01/The%20Northern%20Corridor%20Time%20Release%20Study.pdf); [WCO](https://www.wcoomd.org/en/media/newsroom/2011/july/cb_pilot_trs.aspx). The WCO commended Nigeria's Time Release Study as key to faster trade — [Guardian NG](https://guardian.ng/business-services/wco-hails-nigerias-time-release-study-as-key-to-faster-safer-trade-in-africa/). Customs IT interoperability and corruption were not covered in what I retrieved.
- Informal cross-border trade: estimates range from about 30-40% of intra-regional trade value in SADC to 50-60% of intra-African trade done by unregistered traders (estimates vary widely); women are up to about 70% of informal cross-border traders — [FAO/tralac](https://www.tralac.org/news/article/11665-harnessing-rather-than-suppressing-informal-trade-can-give-africa-a-boost.html); [IPS](https://www.ipsnews.net/?p=150602); [UN Women factsheet](https://knowledge.unwomen.org/sites/default/files/Headquarters/Media/Publications/en/factsheetafricanwomentradersen.pdf).
- Business awareness: ITC reports over half of African MSMEs unaware of AfCFTA; in Kenya, 67% of exporting and 80% of importing firms were unaware (2024 baseline/2025 endline; the summary wording is garbled, so reread the ITC source); an earlier ITC survey of 2,557 firms in francophone Africa (May-July 2021) found only about a quarter had heard of AfCFTA — [Daily News TZ](https://dailynews.co.tz/itc-50pc-of-msmes-unaware-of-afcfta/); [ITC](https://www.intracen.org/es/node/170829); [The EastAfrican](https://theeastafrican.co.ke/tea/business-tech/afcfta-border-hurdles-dampen-free-trade-deal-expectations-3837782).
- RoO and preference use: preferences may go unused when volumes are too small to offset compliance costs, with fixed RoO compliance costs weighing most on small firms; intra-African value chains are thin, so sourcing originating inputs is hard — [UNCTAD, "Simpler rules of origin needed"](https://unctad.org/news/simpler-rules-origin-needed-boost-free-trade-africa-study-shows); [Journal of African Trade](https://www.atlantis-press.com/journals/jat/125947880/view).

**Industrial capacity, diversification and value chains**
- UNCTAD: 46 of Africa's 54 countries are highly commodity-dependent (date unknown) — [VON](https://von.gov.ng/?p=151883). Regional value-chain integration is only 0.5-3% of trade in Africa's regional blocs versus 17.2% (ASEAN) and 4.6% (MERCOSUR) (source/date unclear) — [Invest-Time](https://invest-time.com/en/africas-commodity-dependence/).
- Capacity disparity named by Secretary-General Mene: some countries can exploit the single market immediately while others lack industrial capability — [Shipping Position](https://shippingposition.com.ng/afcfta-implementation-hindered-by-ratification-delays-capacity-disparity-infrastructure-challenges-sec-gen/).

**Trade-revenue dependence and fiscal losses**
- Trade-tax revenue is about 34% of total revenue in African LDCs (year/source via search summary); LDCs are over half of Africa's 54 states — [Afronomics Law](https://www.afronomicslaw.org/2020/04/14/preserving-africas-fiscal-sustainability-in-the-wake-of-the-africa-continental-free-trade-area-agreement).
- IMF-type estimates: removing all tariffs on intraregional imports would cost about 0.3% of GDP on average; for most countries customs revenue losses are below 0.2% of total government revenue; losses are small in absolute terms for Malawi, Liberia, CAR and Sierra Leone but large as a share of their revenue; Côte d'Ivoire, Malawi, Zambia and Zimbabwe have intraregional imports above 35% of total imports (higher exposure) — [Afronomics Law](https://www.afronomicslaw.org/print/pdf/node/1073); [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/mitigating-short-term-adjustment-costs-preparing-for-the-afcfta/). Other estimates: US$4.1 billion tariff revenue loss for Africa; East Africa about US$160 million forfeited in tax revenue; Togo 22.67% of customs revenue in the first three years — [The EastAfrican](https://www.theeastafrican.co.ke/tea/business-tech/ea-faces-160m-forfeiture-in-tax-revenues-under-afcfta-4974722); [JUTA Journals](https://www.jutajournals.co.za/impact-of-the-afcfta-agreement-on-customs-revenue-case-of-togo). A Sept 2025 IGC working paper (Edwards, Chien, Kumwenda, Hakizimana, Rwagasana) and press coverage conclude customs revenue losses under AfCFTA are insignificant — [IGC](https://www.theigc.org/sites/default/files/2025-12/Edwards-Chien-Kumwenda-Hakizimzna-Rwagasana-Working-Paper-September-2025%20.pdf); [The Reporter Ethiopia](https://www.thereporterethiopia.com/38819/). DISCREPANCY: the aggregate-loss estimates range from "minor" to "1-5% of GDP" for individual countries.

**Currency convertibility, payments and trade finance**
- Africa has 42 currencies, with convertibility costs estimated at US$5 billion a year; most intra-African payments clear via USD/EUR. Trade-finance gap US$80-120 billion a year (ICC US$110-120 billion; Afreximbank ATR 2025 cites about US$100 billion) — [Economic Confidential](https://economicconfidential.com/africa-currency-conversion-afcfta/); [The EastAfrican](https://theeastafrican.co.ke/tea/business-tech/afreximbank-banks-on-intra-african-trade-5524444); [Capital FM Africa](https://capitalfm.africa/afcfta-implementation-hurdles-threaten-gains-for-african-businesses/); [Habari Network (ATR 2025)](https://www.thehabarinetwork.com/afreximbank-2025-trade-report-reveals-surge-in-intra-african-commerce-amid-global-shifts). UNECA expects PAPSS to cut FX costs 20-30% — [Ecofin](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news/1104-54591-intra-african-trade-set-to-grow-10-in-2026-as-afcfta-implementation-accelerates).

**Security and disparities**
- Conflicts in the Sahel, eastern DRC and Sudan are described as impeding AfCFTA gains; ISS argues AfCFTA could in turn support peace — [RPublc](https://rpublc.com/vol9-no2/economic-uncertainties-of-the-afcfta); [ISS PSC Report](https://issafrica.org/pscreport/psc-insights/africas-free-trade-initiative-could-bolster-continental-peace). Mali, Niger and Burkina Faso (AES) left ECOWAS (2025) and imposed a 0.5% levy on imports from outside the three countries, including ECOWAS goods; as landlocked states reliant on ECOWAS ports they face higher import costs — [BusinessDay NG](https://businessday.ng/maritime/article/sahel-blocs-import-tariff-on-ecowas-threatens-west-africa-trade-afcfta/); [Guardian NG editorial](https://guardian.ng/opinion/editorial/rebuilding-ecowas-amid-aes-tariff-shock/). Specific evidence on Cabo Delgado, Ethiopia and piracy was not retrieved.
- Concentration: South Africa 19.2% of intra-African trade; top three 30.81% (see Section 2). Winners/losers by country (South Africa/Egypt/Morocco/Kenya/Nigeria versus LDCs): only the generic disparity statements above were retrieved.

### Inferences
- Binding constraints cluster around (i) behind-the-border logistics and regulation, (ii) firm-level awareness and RoO compliance, (iii) thin value chains, and (iv) payments/finance. Tariffs, the AfCFTA's main instrument, are comparatively minor for most large traders.
- Fiscal fear is a political-economy brake (LDC trade taxes about 34% of revenue) even though aggregate losses are small; this helps explain G6 backloading and slow LDC schedule gazetting (e.g., Malawi).
- The rising EAC NTB resolution time (76 to 274 days) shows regional NTB mechanisms are not clearing faster as AfCFTA nears operation.

### Gaps
- No data found on customs IT interoperability (e.g., ASYCUDA, single window integration) or corruption indicators; no ITC SME Competitiveness Outlook 2025/26 text.
- No World Bank/tralac ranking of binding obstacles from firm surveys.
- No data on the AfCFTA Business Forum or Trade Remedies Instrument.
- Cabo Delgado, Ethiopia, piracy specifics: not retrieved.

## 4. What are the most credible estimates of infrastructure and trade-facilitation costs and of time/cost to cross borders in Africa versus other regions?

### Takeaway
The standard citations are dated: AfDB's infrastructure need of US$130-170 billion a year and financing gap of US$68-108 billion (2018 African Economic Outlook, still repeated by the AfCFTA Secretary-General), and World Bank trade-across-borders figures from the early 2010s. The newer 2023 LPI and 2026 World Bank "behind the border" analysis point the same way but I could not retrieve an up-to-date numeric benchmark against other regions.

### Cited Findings
- AfDB: Africa's infrastructure needs are US$130-170 billion a year with a financing gap of US$68-108 billion; AfDB committed over US$44 billion to infrastructure over six years (as of about 2018); only 0.03-0.04% of the US$103 trillion held by institutional investors would close the gap — [BusinessDay NG](https://businessday.ng/business-economy/article/africa-infrastructure-financing-gap/); [VON](https://von.gov.ng/?p=128867); [The Cable](https://www.thecable.ng/?p=692738); origin in [African Economic Outlook 2018 (AfDB)](https://icafrica.org/en/knowledge-hub/article/african-economic-outlook-2018-351). The same range is cited by AfCFTA Secretary-General Mene as an obstacle — [Shipping Position](https://shippingposition.com.ng/afcfta-implementation-hindered-by-ratification-delays-capacity-disparity-infrastructure-challenges-sec-gen/). I did not find a 2025 or 2026 AfDB AEO update in my results.
- World Bank trading-across-borders data (early 2010s Doing Business series, not dated in the summary): Africa takes 31 days to export and 38 days to import, the longest of any region; exporters need about 50% more time than East Asia; cost to ship a container is US$2,108 (export) and US$2,793 (import), about 2.5x and 3.2x East Asia and the Pacific; poor transport infrastructure accounts for about 40% of logistics costs in coastal countries and 60% in landlocked countries — [World Bank Doing Business 2013 chapter](https://subnational.doingbusiness.org/content/dam/doingBusiness/media/Annual-Reports/English/DB13-Chapters/Trading-across-borders.pdf); [Financial Times (Sri Lanka) summary "Barriers blunt trade within Africa"](https://www.ft.lk/International/barriers-blunt-trade-within-africa-world-bank/49-70416); [OECD Aid for Trade](https://t4.oecd.org/aidfortrade/48323804.pdf). The "2.47 versus 3.07" LPI comparison is from 2014.
- World Bank LPI 2023 (released 20 April 2023, 139 countries; 30 African countries ranked): South Africa top in Africa at 3.7 out of 5; Nigeria and Ghana about 2.5-2.6; Nigeria ranked 95th — [Intelpoint](https://intelpoint.co/insights/out-of-30-african-countries-ranked-south-africa-topped-logistics-performance-in-2023-with-a-score-of-3-7-out-of-5/); [Engineering News](https://engineeringnews.co.za/article/logistics-performance-index-2023-05-05); [Shipping Position](https://shippingposition.com.ng/logistics-performance-index-shocker-south-africa-egypt-benin-republic-others-beat-nigeria-to-95th-position-in-latest-ranking/). DISCREPANCY: another summary gives South Africa 3.4 and 19th globally; the 3.7 figure is the better supported one, but verify against the World Bank LPI database.
- World Bank 2026: roughly 60% of Africa's trade costs are behind the border (see Section 3).
- Corridor evidence: Lagos-Abidjan, up to 17 days and 37 checkpoints (Section 3).

### Inferences

## 5. Which gaps are institutional (Secretariat capacity, funding) versus political?

### Takeaway
Institutional constraints are real but second-order: the Secretariat is under-resourced and heavily donor/partner-reliant, while the largest shortfalls in implementation (gazetting, customs systems, ratification of Phase II protocols, NTB removal) sit with national governments, which is a political and administrative rather than Secretariat issue. Political episodes (Nigeria's border closure, AES exit from ECOWAS) show how national interests override continental rules.

### Cited Findings
**Institutional (Secretariat, funding, national structures)**
- Funding: the larger part of the Secretariat's operating budget comes from AU member states, supplemented by the EU, GIZ and Denmark; the African Development Fund approved US$11.02 million in support; Ghana, as host, committed US$10 million for set-up — [New Times Rwanda](https://www.newtimes.co.rw/business/afcfta-secretariat-gets-11m-grant-enhance-effective-implementation); [BFT Online](https://thebftonline.com/?p=108067); [Graphic](https://www.graphic.com.gh/news/politics/parliament-approves-afcfta-secretariat-agreement.html).
- Staffing: Phase 1 staffing was the Secretary-General, four Directors and essential staff; the AU asked for the 104 posts earmarked in 2022 to be cut to what the budget could support; the conferencing division was unstaffed (AU Executive Council 2022 decision) — [AfricanLII, EC decision Feb 2022](https://africanlii.org/akn/aa-au/statement/statement/au-ec/2022/1143/eng@2022-02-03). The summary also states "the $2.9 million budget allocated for 2021 has never been released" (attribution to a specific page is uncertain; verify before using).
- AU-wide arrears: by July 2025 only US$146 million of a US$200 million AU budget had been paid, with some members more than a year in arrears (South Africa and 30 others up to date) — [PMG parliamentary reply NW7066](https://pmg.org.za/committee-question/35102/).
- Adjustment cost: the estimated requirement to fund adjustment costs is about US$10 billion over six years (date unknown, about 2021-22) — [AfricanLII](https://africanlii.org/akn/aa-au/statement/statement/au-ec/2022/1143/eng@2022-02-03). The Adjustment Fund is US$1 billion (Section 1).
- Reform: AfCFTA's chief has called for reforming the Secretariat to raise efficiency (date unknown) — [TradeMark Africa](https://www.trademarkafrica.com/news/afcftas-chief-calls-for-reforming-the-secretariat-to-increase-efficiency). New political oversight: the Committee of Heads of State and Government (Feb 2026).
- National structures: UNECA says 36 countries completed NIS; ECA/AfCFTA capacity programmes continue (Section 1). Secretary-General Mene lists ratification delays, capacity disparities, transport infrastructure, customs inefficiency and slow ratification of the Protocol on Free Movement of Persons among obstacles — [Shipping Position](https://shippingposition.com.ng/afcfta-implementation-hindered-by-ratification-delays-capacity-disparity-infrastructure-challenges-sec-gen/); [The Citizen TZ](https://thecitizen.co.tz/tanzania/news/africa/afcfta-chief-focus-must-shift-from-agreements-to-action-5097060).

**Political**
- Nigeria closed land borders with Benin, Cameroon, Chad and Niger in Aug 2019 (Seme closed 19 Aug 2019), reopening after about 16 months (i.e., by end-2020) with "very little gain" (Nigerian press assessment); the World Bank estimated about 80% of Benin's imports are destined for Nigeria; analysts said the closure violated ECOWAS and AfCFTA commitments — [ISS Today](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/nigerias-border-closures-havent-served-their-purpose); [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/nigerias-benin-border-closure-and-what-it-means-for-the-african-continental-free-trade-agreement-afcfta/); [Guardian NG](https://guardian.ng/?p=1290653); [BusinessDay NG](https://businessday.ng/exclusives/article/one-year-after-a-tale-of-suffering-behind-nigerias-closed-borders/).
- Nigeria's comparatively slow move to implementation: it was not among the GTI's first eight participants; schedule gazetted Apr 2025, recognised in South Africa's tariff notice Sept 2025, rollout reported Nov 2025. A trade-press headline from the GTI launch period refers to "denials and excuses by Nigerian officials" as Ghana, Kenya and Rwanda began trading, and a ministry statement says Nigeria is "ready and equipped" (article bodies not read; dates unknown) — [BusinessDay NG](https://businessday.ng/maritime/article/ministry-says-nigeria-is-ready-equipped-to-trade-in-afcfta-market/); [Shipping Position](https://shippingposition.com.ng/amidst-denials-excuses-by-nigerian-officials-ghana-kenya-rwanda-begin-trading-on-afcfta-portal/).
- Heads of State push in Feb 2026 (Ruto: urgent action) and AU request for Phase II ratification by end-2026 (Section 1). The AES exit and levy (Section 3). SADC Parliamentary Forum concerned about unratified states — [SADC PF](https://www.parliament.gov.za/news/sadc-pf-concerned-some-member-states-are-yet-ratify-africa-free-trade-agreement).
- South African and Nigerian politics (sovereignty/protectionist interests): the material retrieved gave only Nigeria's border-closure episode and South Africa's role as the largest intra-African trader; no direct sources on domestic protectionist lobbies were retrieved.

### Inferences
- The Secretariat's budget and staffing constraints limit monitoring, technical support and enforcement (e.g., NTB resolution), but the binding step to trade (national gazetting, customs systems, RoO administration, ratification) is controlled by national governments; the World Bank's governance point ("binding commitments not matched by enforcement machinery") supports this.
- The fiscal dependence of LDCs and the interests of large producers explain why liberalisation is back-loaded and why sensitive/excluded lists and RoO (automotive, textiles) took until 2025-26 to settle.
- Political shocks (Nigeria 2019-20, AES 2025) show that continental commitments are weakly enforced; there is no evidence of any sanction being applied.

### Gaps
- No up-to-date (2025/2026) Secretariat budget, headcount, member-state contribution or donor share figures. The 2021 US$2.9 million figure is unverified and old.
- No sources on domestic politics in South Africa/Nigeria (trade unions, manufacturers' lobbying) or on sovereignty arguments.
- No evidence on dispute settlement actually being used.
- Security specifics (Cabo Delgado, Sudan, Ethiopia, piracy) not retrieved; search budget exhausted before these were run.
