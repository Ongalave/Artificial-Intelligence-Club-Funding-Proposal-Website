Executive Summary

AI literacy is no longer optional—it is a foundational professional skill.

As Hampden-Sydney prepares its approximately 1,000 students to lead in competitive fields including finance, law, public policy, and business, the ability to understand and apply AI tools will increasingly define who succeeds. The question is not whether H-SC engages with AI—it is whether the college leads or follows.

Among ODAC peer institutions and comparable liberal arts colleges, no school has established a dedicated AI initiative. This proposal represents a first-mover opportunity to position Hampden-Sydney as a distinctive leader—not by abandoning the liberal arts, but by demonstrating that a liberal arts education is more valuable in the age of AI, not less.

The H-SC AI Club has already proven this model works: 34 active members, two shipped applications serving the campus, and direct mentorship from Sydney Henriques (H-SC '09), who heads the Center for Excellence in Artificial Intelligence at J.P. Morgan Chase. This proposal scales that proof of concept into a college-wide capability over three semesters, with two investment tiers designed to let the Board choose its level of commitment.

The Liberal Arts AI Advantage

The students who thrive in an AI-driven economy will not be the ones who can prompt a chatbot.

They will be the ones who can think critically about what to ask, evaluate whether the answer is true, communicate it persuasively, and reason about whether it is ethical. That is a Hampden-Sydney education.

AI does not replace the liberal arts—it makes them essential. As AI tools become ubiquitous, the differentiating skill set shifts from technical operation to human judgment. Every field H-SC graduates enter—finance, law, policy, business—will be reshaped by AI. The graduates who lead will be those who understand both the technology and the human context in which it operates.

H-SC Disciplines × AI Competencies

Rhetoric

Prompt engineering, output evaluation, persuasive communication with and about AI systems

Ethics

Responsible AI deployment, bias detection, governance frameworks for automated decision-making

Economics & Business

Quantitative AI applications, market analysis, financial modeling with machine learning

Government

AI policy, regulation, civil liberties implications, democratic governance of technology

History

Pattern recognition, contextualizing technological disruption, institutional memory

Mathematics

Statistical foundations, model architecture, data analysis and validation

The AI Club's planned workshops will be open to students across all disciplines—not siloed in a technical department. The initiative welcomes faculty participation, offering resources, training opportunities, and co-development partnerships for professors interested in exploring AI applications within their disciplines.

Demonstrated Impact

The H-SC AI Club was founded with a mission to bring applied artificial intelligence education and project development to Hampden-Sydney College. With the direct support and mentorship of Sydney Henriques (H-SC '09), who heads the Center for Excellence in Artificial Intelligence at J.P. Morgan Chase, the club has rapidly evolved from a student initiative into one of the most technically ambitious organizations on campus.
